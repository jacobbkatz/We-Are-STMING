B — continuity at the lead free ends

Unpowered, meter on beep. The four jumper leads go into JP1 holes 1, 2, 3 and 5; this checks each one arrives where it should and touches nothing else.

C — the smoke test

Set the supply before anything is connected to it. Supply off, both channels to 5.0 V, current limit 20 mA, then meter each output to confirm. A supply you have not metered is not a supply you have set.

  1. Make a ± supply. Channel A's − terminal joined to channel B's + terminal — that junction is your ground.

  2. Connect, in this order: ground → GND lead. A+ → +15 V lead. B− → −15 V lead. OUT lead taped off, touching nothing.

  3. Switch on. Both channels should read a few mA, within 1 mA of each other. The op-amp's supply current flows in one rail and out the other, so the two match. Extra current on the −15 V channel alone means C2 is leaking.

  4. Off immediately if a channel sits at the 20 mA limit, the current climbs rather than settling, or anything is warm to the touch.

  5. Then 15.0 V for ten minutes. Never higher — the op-amp's absolute maximum is ±18 V. Recheck both currents and touch C2.

  6. Disconnect −15 V first, then +15 V, then ground.

PAD1 will sit near a supply rail throughout, and that is correct. The 100 MΩ feedback resistor is not fitted yet, so the amplifier has no feedback path. This test is about current and heat, nothing else.

D0 — the standoff

Correction, same evening. This page first said the standoff was a press fit with 0.076 mm of interference, and offered a #44 drill as the remedy. That was wrong, and drilling would have made the hole worse. Berard’s own board file — in this repository since August — specifies drill="2.1082" for this exact hole, and our board is 2.108 mm. He used an 11301 in it. Corrected again 2026-09-15, at the bench: it does NOT enter the hole. The clearance figure was the third wrong answer on this part number, and the first to have a file in this repository agreeing with it. The node was built with the standoff resting on the board surface. Still do not drill anything.

Then seat it by hand. It should drop through or take a light push. The PTFE base sits flat on the board surface with the metal pole standing proud above it.

If it will not go: stop and tell me. Do not force it, do not drill. FR4 is brittle and this hole is 2.54 mm from the board edge — and it is the insulator for the input node of a 100 MΩ amplifier, where a radial crack is a leakage path and a contamination trap in the 100 MΩ to 10 GΩ range a meter cannot see. If it does not fit, something about the part is not what we think, and that is worth an hour of my time rather than two minutes of yours.

The part is PTFE-based with metal only on the pole — confirmed at the bench. A top-mount insulated terminal, so the input node gets a real PTFE insulator, which is exactly what Berard was doing. Two in hand, so there is a spare.

D — wash, then build the node

Gate CLEARED — 99% IPA, DI water, foam swabs and a soft brush are all in the room. 91% is not enough here, which is why the 99% mattered.

  1. Wash the whole board. Scrub with IPA → rinse with clean IPA → rinse with deionised water → dry at 60–70 °C for 1–2 hours if you have a dehydrator or oven that holds it, otherwise overnight.

  2. Press in the standoff, if D0 left it for now.

  3. Air-mount the 100 MΩ from PAD1 (the op-amp output) to the standoff. Do not shorten its leads and do not lay it flat against the board — the air gap is the point.

  4. A slack 40 AWG link from the standoff to IC1 pin 2's pad toe. Slack, so nothing pulls on the joint.

  5. Clean locally with swabs and IPA, then dry again.

D5 — the measurement this has all been for

Bench supply ±15 V, 20 mA limits. Meter on the OUT lead, which reads the same node as PAD1. No box, no tip lead, and nobody within a metre — a body injects tens of nA and a tunnelling current is about 1 nA.

Readings at 1, 5, 10, 20, 45 and 60 minutes, recording both channel currents each time. 45 minutes is the one that counts — the offset climbed for over an hour after power-on on the old board, and an early reading looks like a spectacular improvement and is worthless.

This is the first preamp reading in this project that measures the preamp. Every previous one was taken with IC1 pin 3 floating, which is why the “119 nA” was never a current. Whatever this number is, it is the first honest one.
