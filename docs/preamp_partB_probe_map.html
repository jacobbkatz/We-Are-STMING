Spare preamp board · unpowered · about 5 minutes

Part B — the four lead free ends

Six readings on the dangling ends of the four jumper leads, before a power supply is ever clipped to them. Meter on continuity. Nothing is powered at any point.

This is not the test you did last night

Last night the black probe sat in hole 1 and the red probe moved around the board. That tested the four repair wires — the thin magnet-wire links from the scraped vias — and they all passed.

This test is at the other end. It probes the cut-off free ends of the four jumper leads, out in the air, back to landmarks on the board. It answers a different question: is each lead the one you think it is, and is it touching any of the others?

That matters because in Part C you clip a bench supply onto those four ends. A lead in the wrong place, or two ends touching, puts a short across the supply and you would read it as a board fault that is not there.

  • 1  +15 V → C1 far
  • 2  −15 V → C2 far
  • 3  GND → C1 near
  • 4  OUT → PAD1

The lead colours in this drawing are not your lead colours. The four leads are cut-off jumper cable and nobody recorded what colour each one is, so they are all drawn the same grey here. Go by the hole number they come out of: 1 GND, 2 +15 V, 3 OUT, 4 empty, 5 −15 V. While you have the meter out, tell me which colour is in which hole and I will write it into docs/INVENTORY.md so nobody has to work it out again.

The six readings

1  +15 V lead free end ↔ C1’s far pad MUST BEEPFar means the pad away from the holes, on the left. This proves the +15 V lead actually reaches C1.
2  −15 V lead free end ↔ C2’s far pad MUST BEEPSame idea on the bottom capacitor. C2 is the one that was rotated, so this also re-confirms its joint.
3  GND lead free end ↔ C1’s near pad MUST BEEPNear means the pad closest to the holes, on the right.
4  OUT lead free end ↔ PAD1 ABOUT 220 ΩNot a beep — a number. That 220 Ω is R1, the series output resistor between IC1 pin 6 and hole 3. A beep here would mean R1 is shorted; an open would mean the OUT lead is not connected.
5  OUT lead ↔ each of the other three leads ALL SILENTThree separate readings: OUT to GND, OUT to +15 V, OUT to −15 V.
6  +15 V ↔ −15 V, and each ↔ GND NO HELD BEEPThree more readings. A brief chirp is a capacitor charging and is a pass — same as holes 2 and 5 last night. Only a beep that holds is a short.

If anything fails, stop here

Do not go on to Part C. A lead that does not reach where it should, or two ends that beep to each other, is a five-minute fix now and a confusing hour once a supply is attached. Send me which row failed and what it read.

Nothing gets powered by anything but the current-limited bench supply, and this board does not meet the controller at all until C2 is replaced and PAD1 reads under 0.1 V at 45 minutes — STATUS.md safety rule 14.

All six as expected? Then Part C, the current-limited smoke test, on the bench page docs/preamp_next_steps.html. Set the supply to 5.0 V and a 20 mA limit and meter both outputs before anything is connected.
