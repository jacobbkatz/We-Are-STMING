## 2. The two firmware faults

### 2.1 `CCON` jumps Z to midscale

Berard, on his coarse approach page:

> When the Z-feedback is switched on, the integral term first must be initialized to the current
> Z-value. Otherwise, the feedback will cause a small jump in the Z-piezo when it's switched on,
> which crashes the tip!

Checked our source. We have it:

```cpp
void turn_on_const_current(int target_adc) {
    this->dac_z_control_value = static_cast<double>(stm_status.dac_z);  // assigned
    pTerm = 0.0; iTerm = 0.0;
}
int control_current(int adc_value) {
    int z = static_cast<int>(pTerm + iTerm) + 32768;   // never reads it. midscale hardcoded
```

`dac_z_control_value` is written and **read nowhere in the codebase** — verified by grep, not
assumed. So the first `control_current()` after `CCON` drives Z to about 32768 regardless of
where Z was.

**Magnitude.** From Z = 15000 that is ~17000 counts. Taking Berard's measured 34 nm/V on a disc
scanner across our ±10 V Z range gives roughly 0.01 nm per count, so about **180 nm of sudden
travel**. Tunneling is under 1 nm. This is derived, not measured, and the direction depends on
where Z happens to be, but the order of magnitude is not in doubt.

**Not fixed.** The fix is to seed `iTerm = stm_status.dac_z - 32768` so the first output equals
the current Z. It changes behaviour of the feedback loop and needs bench testing before it is
trusted, so it is recorded rather than applied.

### 2.2 The motor is never de-energised

`EfficientStepper::step()` calls `enable()` and never disables. `disable()` appears in exactly one
place, inside `approach()`, on success only. After any `MTMV` all four coils stay driven.

Berard: *"The motor produces a substantial amount of heat, which can cause the scanner to drift out
of range within minutes."* He also runs his motor at 3.3 V rather than 5 V for the same reason.

The 28BYJ-48 is geared and holds position without holding current, which is the whole reason a
geared motor was chosen. **Not fixed**, same reasoning.

**This is the prime suspect if drift ever shows up minutes into a session.**

## 3. Coarse approach step size

`sessions/2026-08-31-plan.md` §0.10 estimated **244 nm/step**, assuming an M3 screw at 0.5 mm pitch
driven directly, and called four steps per Z range "tight".

Both assumptions were wrong.

| | |
|---|---|
| Screw is 1/4"-80, per `BOM.md` | 25.4 / 80 = **317.5 µm/rev** |
| Steps per revolution, measured on our motor | **2048** |
| At the screw | **155 nm/step** |
| Berard's lever reduction, his geometry | **~20x** |
| **At the tip, if ours is similar** | **≈ 7.8 nm/step** |

Berard explains where the 20 comes from: the rear approach screw sits 20 mm from the scanner and
the two front screws about 1 mm along the same axis, so the tip moves ~20x less than the screw.
Ordinary 3-screw lever geometry.

Against a Z range of roughly 700 nm that is about **90 steps per Z range** instead of four. The
approach is far safer than the project believed.

**VERIFY: our lever ratio is not measured.** Ours is Mech Panda's scan head, not Berard's. It is
measurable from `CAD/prints/scan-head/`. Even at the worst case of no lever at all, 155 nm/step
against 700 nm still works.

Also noted: Berard states his fine screw moves 250 µm/rev while elsewhere confirming the screws are
1/4-80, which is 317.5 µm/rev. His own figures disagree slightly. We used the pitch arithmetic.

## 6. Licensing

Jacob's requirement: showable to professors, not fully public, and leaving the door open to
commercialising far down the road.

**Recommendation, recorded rather than acted on: keep the repository private and add no
open-source licence for now.** An added licence is effectively irreversible, because anyone who
takes a copy under it keeps those rights forever. Private plus no licence preserves every option,
and a private repository can still be shown to a professor by inviting them or sending a link.

**The thing that actually needs checking first is upstream.** This project is a derivative of Mech
Panda's `red-panda-stm`. Whatever licence that carries governs what we may do commercially with
the parts derived from it, and nobody has looked. Berard's preamplifier files are MIT, which is
permissive and commercially fine with attribution. **Mech Panda's licence is unknown and is the
binding constraint.** That is now the open question, not which licence to pick.

Not legal advice, and worth a real answer before anyone builds a business plan on it.

## 9. The `CCON` bug is upstream, not ours

The uploaded `stm_app_2.py` is the **PC-side GUI in Python**; the bug is in the **Teensy firmware
in C++**. Different layer, so the upload neither confirmed nor contradicted it. Checked anyway:

- **The uploaded file is byte-identical to `Code/pc/stm_app.py`** after normalising line endings
  (CRLF vs LF; md5 `052f0edf...` both ways). The repository is in sync.
- **Mech Panda's repository was cloned** from `github.com/MechRedPanda/red-panda-stm`. The bug is
  present there, unchanged. `dac_z_control_value` appears exactly three times in his whole `Code/`
  tree: the declaration, **a commented-out PID line**, and the assignment. It is never read.

That commented-out line is how the bug happened:

```cpp
// PID current_pid = PID(&adc_real_value_log_log, &dac_z_control_value, ...)
```

`dac_z_control_value` was the output variable for an Arduino PID library. The library was replaced
with hand-rolled P+I code, `+ 32768` was hardcoded in its place, and the variable was left
orphaned. A refactor that dropped a thread, not carelessness.

**`EfficientStepper.cpp` is also byte-identical to his**, so the motor-left-energised issue is
upstream too, not something we introduced.

Our firmware differs from his in exactly three places, all ours: `play_tone()`, the ADC clock drop
to 1 MHz, and the `TONE` command in `main.cpp`. `stm_control.py` is identical.

**Caveat recorded honestly:** this analysis is of the firmware *as committed*. If Nuh has flashed
something newer that was never pushed, it could be stale. Worth him confirming.

## 11. `stm_approach.py`, written and tested

The GUI's Approach button was **not** the tool that had been proposed. `stm_control.approach()` is
three lines that send `APRH` to the firmware — a remote control for the buggy firmware path, with
no approach logic of its own. Mech Panda has no approach script either; his `Code/pc/` holds only
`stm_app.py`, `stm_control.py` and `stm_control_test.py`.

**Written this session:** `Code/pc/stm_approach.py`, a PC-side woodpecker loop.

| Property | Why |
|---|---|
| **Never sends `APRH`** | Asserted by a test that inspects every command issued |
| Thresholds on **absolute deviation** from a measured baseline | Works whichever way tunneling moves the reading. This is the firmware's actual defect |
| **Motor only moves while Z is retracted** | The woodpecker invariant. Tested by replaying the whole command stream across four gap sizes and both Z orientations |
| **Refuses to start if the ADC is railed** | At today's ~29873 counts there is no headroom for a threshold to be crossed, so an approach could never trigger and the motor would run to its limit |
| **Requires both direction answers** | `--z-retracted` and `--motor-toward-sample` are required arguments with no defaults. Getting either backwards turns a safe approach into a drive into the sample |
| **Retracts Z on every exit path** | A `finally` block. Tested against success, step-limit, an exception mid-run, and Ctrl-C |
| Step budget is exact | Default 200 steps, about 1.6 um |
| `--dry-run` | Everything except moving the motor |

**Testing.** `Code/pc/stm_approach_test.py`, **40 tests, all passing**, against a simulated
microscope that models a tip closing on a sample. Two findings came out of the tests themselves:

- A **step-budget overrun**: with `--motor-step 3` and `--max-steps 7` the original code sent 9
  steps. Fixed by clamping the final move; the budget is now exact.
- One test failed and **the code was right, the test was wrong** — it assumed a motor move had
  happened in a case where the piezo could reach the sample unaided. Corrected the test.

**This has never touched hardware.** It is written to fail safe, and it currently refuses to run at
all because the preamp is railed, but nothing in it is bench-proven.

## 15. The ADC full scale is 4.096 V. The question is effectively closed.

**The controller schematic has been sitting in our own `PCB/` directory the whole time.** Most PDF
tools extract nothing from it because Altium/EasyEDA exported the text as vector outlines. PyMuPDF
reads it fine. Nobody had tried.

Page 2 is the ADC sheet, and it settles the 4.096 versus 10.24 question:

| Evidence | |
|---|---|
| U15 LTC2326HMS-16, with pins **REFIN (8)** and **REFBUF (7)** | schematic p2 |
| **"VREF1" and "ADR421" appear zero times on the ADC sheet** | searched the extracted text |
| U5 ADR421 drives VREF1 to the four AD5761 pin-4s **and nowhere else** | schematic p1 |
| Berard's constant is `_ref_buffer_volts = 4.096f`, named after the REFBUF pin | `LTC2326_16.hpp` |
| R23 probe on 2026-08-31 implied a full scale near 4-5 V | `2026-08-31-results.md` §3.8 |

The ADC runs on its **internal reference**. 4.096 is right; the 10.24 in `stm_control.py:37` and
`stm_console.py` is wrong and does not correspond to anything on this board.

**Every current figure those tools have ever printed is 2.5x too large.**

**Deliberately not changed.** Editing that constant would silently alter every number the tools
produce, and it should land together with the calibration that proves it. Two things remain: the
datasheet's full-scale-to-REFBUF relationship, and one simultaneous R23-versus-`ADCR` reading.

## 18. What other DIY STM builders do — `docs/OTHER_BUILDERS.md`

The useful one, because it addresses an UNKNOWN that has already destroyed hardware:

**`jherkenhoff/STM` attaches the piezo wires with conductive epoxy (MG Chemicals 9410) instead of
soldering**, specifically to avoid depolarising the ceramic. Manufacturer handling guidance
corroborates: **depolarisation starts above about 210 °C** internal temperature, and conductive
adhesive is a normal alternative to solder.

Our Sn42/Bi58 low-temp paste approach stands and is not replaced. The epoxy is recorded as a
second option that removes the heat entirely. **Untested by us.**

Also recorded: quadrant cutting is done by scraping only the **silver electrode**, not the ceramic
beneath (scalpel, or ruler and razor; Berard prefers a diamond scribe to an X-acto). Another
builder independently chose a high-resistivity ceramic standoff for the same leakage reason we
chose PTFE. Two other builds use Viton O-rings in their damping stacks. The unimorph disc scanner
is **John Alexander's** invention, upstream of Berard.
