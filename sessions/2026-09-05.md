## 2. The two firmware faults

### 2.1 `CCON` jumps Z to midscale

Berard, on his coarse approach page:

> When the Z-feedback is switched on, the integral term first must be initialized to the current
> Z-value. Otherwise, the feedback will cause a small jump in the Z-piezo when it's switched on,
> which crashes the tip!

Checked our source. We have it:

```cpp
void turn_on_const_current(int target_adc) {
    this->dac_z_control_value = static_cast<double>(stm_status.dac_z);  // assigned
    pTerm = 0.0; iTerm = 0.0;
}
int control_current(int adc_value) {
    int z = static_cast<int>(pTerm + iTerm) + 32768;   // never reads it. midscale hardcoded
```

`dac_z_control_value` is written and **read nowhere in the codebase** — verified by grep, not
assumed. So the first `control_current()` after `CCON` drives Z to about 32768 regardless of
where Z was.

**Magnitude.** From Z = 15000 that is ~17000 counts. Taking Berard's measured 34 nm/V on a disc
scanner across our ±10 V Z range gives roughly 0.01 nm per count, so about **180 nm of sudden
travel**. Tunneling is under 1 nm. This is derived, not measured, and the direction depends on
where Z happens to be, but the order of magnitude is not in doubt.

**Not fixed.** The fix is to seed `iTerm = stm_status.dac_z - 32768` so the first output equals
the current Z. It changes behaviour of the feedback loop and needs bench testing before it is
trusted, so it is recorded rather than applied.

### 2.2 The motor is never de-energised

`EfficientStepper::step()` calls `enable()` and never disables. `disable()` appears in exactly one
place, inside `approach()`, on success only. After any `MTMV` all four coils stay driven.

Berard: *"The motor produces a substantial amount of heat, which can cause the scanner to drift out
of range within minutes."* He also runs his motor at 3.3 V rather than 5 V for the same reason.

The 28BYJ-48 is geared and holds position without holding current, which is the whole reason a
geared motor was chosen. **Not fixed**, same reasoning.

**This is the prime suspect if drift ever shows up minutes into a session.**

## 3. Coarse approach step size

`sessions/2026-08-31-plan.md` §0.10 estimated **244 nm/step**, assuming an M3 screw at 0.5 mm pitch
driven directly, and called four steps per Z range "tight".

Both assumptions were wrong.

| | |
|---|---|
| Screw is 1/4"-80, per `BOM.md` | 25.4 / 80 = **317.5 µm/rev** |
| Steps per revolution, measured on our motor | **2048** |
| At the screw | **155 nm/step** |
| Berard's lever reduction, his geometry | **~20x** |
| **At the tip, if ours is similar** | **≈ 7.8 nm/step** |

Berard explains where the 20 comes from: the rear approach screw sits 20 mm from the scanner and
the two front screws about 1 mm along the same axis, so the tip moves ~20x less than the screw.
Ordinary 3-screw lever geometry.

Against a Z range of roughly 700 nm that is about **90 steps per Z range** instead of four. The
approach is far safer than the project believed.

**VERIFY: our lever ratio is not measured.** Ours is Mech Panda's scan head, not Berard's. It is
measurable from `CAD/prints/scan-head/`. Even at the worst case of no lever at all, 155 nm/step
against 700 nm still works.

Also noted: Berard states his fine screw moves 250 µm/rev while elsewhere confirming the screws are
1/4-80, which is 317.5 µm/rev. His own figures disagree slightly. We used the pitch arithmetic.

## 5. What was deliberately not concluded

- **Neither firmware fix was applied.** Both change behaviour that can move a tip, and neither can
  be tested from here. They are documented with the proposed patch so Nuh can apply and test them
  at the bench.
- **The coax versus 40 AWG question was not resolved in the BOM.** Coax buys shielding on the
  input line; fine wire buys mechanical isolation and lower capacitance. Berard chose wire. We have
  tested neither, so both are recorded.
- **The lever ratio was not measured**, only borrowed from Berard with a VERIFY. Measuring it from
  the STL geometry is a genuine remote task and was not done this session.
- **No LICENSE was added.** See below.

## 6. Licensing

Jacob's requirement: showable to professors, not fully public, and leaving the door open to
commercialising far down the road.

**Recommendation, recorded rather than acted on: keep the repository private and add no
open-source licence for now.** An added licence is effectively irreversible, because anyone who
takes a copy under it keeps those rights forever. Private plus no licence preserves every option,
and a private repository can still be shown to a professor by inviting them or sending a link.

**The thing that actually needs checking first is upstream.** This project is a derivative of Mech
Panda's `red-panda-stm`. Whatever licence that carries governs what we may do commercially with
the parts derived from it, and nobody has looked. Berard's preamplifier files are MIT, which is
permissive and commercially fine with attribution. **Mech Panda's licence is unknown and is the
binding constraint.** That is now the open question, not which licence to pick.

Not legal advice, and worth a real answer before anyone builds a business plan on it.
