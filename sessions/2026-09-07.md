## 4. New faults

### 4.1 The PTFE standoff is glued to the board with cyanoacrylate

**Reported by Jacob today.** The standoff would not fit through the board hole, so a small amount
of CA was used to stick it down.

The standoff exists to hold the input node off the board surface, because PTFE is about the best
practical insulator there is. **A CA bond at its base puts a conductive-when-damp bridge straight
across that insulator:**

```
input node  →  down the standoff  →  CA joint at the base  →  board surface  →  a supply rail
```

`sessions/2026-08-31-results.md` already says cured CA "is hygroscopic and conducts at exactly the
level measured". A few hundred megohms is all that is needed, and the joint is at 0 mm from the
node rather than the 4.8 mm of the box glue.

**Why the sign matters.** For a transimpedance amplifier, a leak to the **negative** rail pulls
current out of the virtual-ground node, and the op-amp must supply it back through the feedback
resistor — which drives the output **positive**. The measured output is **+3.73 V, positive**.
So the leak is to the negative rail, and `IC1` pin 4 is −15 V at 2.54 mm.

**Two honest caveats.** This rests on the ADC reading being signed two's complement, which is still
INFERRED (`docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md` §5) — although if the rail test agrees, that would
independently settle the signed question. And `IC1` pin 3 is GND, sitting directly between pins 2
and 4, which acts as an accidental guard against a straight-line surface path. So the leak more
likely goes over or around the pins — which is exactly what a blob of glue and a standoff would do.

### 4.2 The board cannot be removed from the box

Heavily superglued to three standoffs that are printed as part of the box. Consequences:

- **Cleaning is partial at best.** The top surface can be cleaned; the CA underneath cannot.
- **Replacing the board means a new box print too.**
- A negative result from a clean-and-remeasure **does not clear the superglue**, because only the
  accessible half would have been cleaned.

### 4.3 The box's standoffs do not match the board's mounting holes

Only **one** of the two screw positions is usable (§3). One screw will not hold a board, which is
almost certainly why it was glued. **This is a defect in the printed part, not a mistake made at
the bench.**

**Fix before the spare board is mounted in anything:** move the threaded standoffs to sit under the
board's real hole positions, 5.93 mm apart, keeping the Ø1.600 mm pilots.

  the entire dangerous range unmeasured. **Do not read OL as "ruled out."** The instrument that can
  see into that range is the rail-scaling test.

# Addendum, same day: three corrections from Jacob, and what they imply

Jacob read the write-up above and corrected three things. The first was my error; the other two
were facts nobody had recorded. Together they **narrow** the diagnosis rather than widening it.

## 10. The bias has been on the whole time

I recorded "no bias, tip in air" as a fact and built an argument on it. **That was wrong.** The
sample plate is wired and biased; it is simply parked away from the tip holder.

**Working out what that implies is what produced the strongest result of the session.** Nothing had
commanded a DAC before the 2026-08-31 capture, so by `docs/DAC_BOOT_STATE.md` every DAC sat at
**zero scale**, and each inverting output stage flips it:

| Source | Where it actually sits | Would drive the preamp output | Matches the measured **+3.73 V**? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sample holder, via bias | DAC −3 V → holder **+3 V** | negative | **No** |
| Piezo electrodes on DSUB1 | DAC −10 V → DSUB1 **+10 V** | negative | **No** |
| Preamp **+15 V** rail | **+15 V** | negative | **No** |
| **Preamp −15 V rail** | **−15 V** | **positive** | **YES** |

A leak from a **positive** source pushes current *into* the virtual-ground node, which drives the
output **negative**. The measured output is **positive**. **So the source must be negative — and
the only negative voltage anywhere near the input node is the preamp's own −15 V rail.**

That is on the preamp board, 2.54 mm from the glued PTFE standoff. **Turning the bias on, in other
words, did not add a suspect. It eliminated one, and it eliminated the piezo drive too.**

**Caveats.** Signed two's complement is still INFERRED. Whether a DAC was commanded earlier in that
session is not recorded. And the hourly configuration loss means the real outputs are never certain.
All three are cheap to close, and all three tests are **zero-risk because no tip is fitted.**

## 11. No tip is fitted — only the tip holder

Two consequences, both good:

- **Nothing can be crashed.** Bias sweeps, Z sweeps and rail scaling are all zero-risk right now.
  That is a window worth using before a tip goes in.
- **The tip holder is still part of the input node**, and it is a bare metal object sitting in air.
  It is an antenna, and its surface and mounting are leakage candidates in their own right.

## 12. The piezo was superglued into its socket — a third CA site

**New fault, and it sits at the scan head next to the tip holder**, which is part of the input node.

`STATUS.md` safety rule 7 has always said to meter the tip holder against the brass piezo electrode
before imaging, on Berard's warning that glue must not bridge the two. **We now know glue was used
exactly there.** That check has stopped being a precaution and become a test of a live candidate
(now candidate E).

**A second, separate risk with this one.** A unimorph disc scanner works by clamping the brass rim
and letting the ceramic centre flex freely into the clearance bore beneath — that is what the
Ø20.500 seat over the Ø18.000 bore is for. **If CA has run under the disc or into that clearance,
the scanner is mechanically stiffened and its travel is reduced or gone.** Nobody would have noticed,
because the scanner has never been watched moving — that needs a working preamp. **Flag it now so it
is not diagnosed as an electrical fault later.** UNKNOWN, and not measurable until the preamp works.

## 14. Three more pieces of context from Jacob

### The fine screws are not the part in the CAD

**Ours are 1/4"-80, same precision, but about 30 mm longer** than the McMaster 97424A590 named in
`CAD/STM.f3z`. That archive is **Mech Panda's** CAD, so its part number describes upstream's screw,
not ours — and the longer screw is why our `MotorSupport` is taller than Mech Panda's.

**The thread is what sets the physics and it is unchanged**, so every nm-per-step figure still
holds. What changes is stiffness and drift: an extra 30 mm of steel in the tip-to-sample loop is
**about 0.33 µm per °C** of extra expansion (11 ppm/K, DERIVED), and a longer screw column is a
softer spring, so the head's resonance drops. Neither is measured and neither blocks anything today.
`docs/BOM.md` corrected. **The real part number still needs finding.**

### Wire colours — the rule, after I got it backwards once

I first wrote this up as "never use colours, always pin numbers". **Jacob corrected it: they prefer
colours.** The actual rule:

**Use the J1 / J2 colours documented in `docs/WIRING.md`. Do not use colours read off a
photograph.** The jumper leads at the preamp end are different colours from the J1/J2 wiring, and
Jacob and Nuh do that translation themselves.

Now in `CLAUDE.md` §7 and at the top of `docs/WIRING.md`. Worth remembering why the photo trap
matters: DSUB2 carries +15 V on pin 5 and −15 V on pin 4, adjacent to the preamp signal pins.

### The coax is buying very little — question answered

Jacob asked how much the coax actually helps, given it runs **1–2 cm and then becomes a single piece
of bare copper wrapping and soldering to the tip holder.**

**Electrically it is close to a wash.** A shield only shields what it covers, and pickup scales with
the exposed length of the high-impedance conductor — so the bare link **plus the tip holder itself**,
a bare metal object in open air, dominates. The cost is small too: 2 cm of RG-178 is only about
**2 pF**.

**Mechanically it may be doing real harm, for a reason that is not the usual one.** The link lands on
the tip holder, which sits on the piezo disc — a unimorph whose centre must flex freely by hundreds
of nanometres. RG-178 is ~1.8 mm across; 40 AWG is 0.08 mm. **Bending stiffness goes as the fourth
power of diameter.** Anything stiff bonded to a flexing piezo loads it and reduces travel — the same
class of problem as the glue at the piezo socket. That is probably the real reason Berard chose
40 AWG, and it is a better argument than the bench-vibration one usually given.

**Explicitly not on the critical path for the 37 nA.** Coax versus wire changes noise and mechanics.
It cannot produce a DC offset — the braid is at ground and the input node is at virtual ground, and
two things at 0 V do not drive current through a path between them.

**What would actually improve the shielding** is getting the whole high-impedance region — tip
holder, bare link and preamp box — inside the grounded scan-head shield cover, which is how Berard's
design gets away with plain wire. Full write-up in `docs/UPSTREAM_BERARD.md` §4.

**One thing to check either way, not currently recorded anywhere: the coax braid should be grounded
at the preamp end only.** Both ends makes a ground loop; neither end makes the shield useless.

## 15. Decision: the coax goes, replaced by plain fine wire

Jacob added that the coax was **difficult to strip and solder**, and asked whether to replace it
with plain copper straight to the tip holder. **Yes.** Four independent reasons:

1. **It buys almost nothing electrically** — 1–2 cm shielded against a bare link plus a bare tip
   holder in open air, and only ~2 pF of added capacitance either way.
2. **It probably loads the piezo disc.** Bending stiffness goes as diameter⁴; the link lands on a
   unimorph that must flex freely by hundreds of nanometres.
3. **It halves the solder joints on the input node** — coax needs centre *and* braid terminated,
   plus a joint to the bare link.
4. **Difficulty at that node is itself a fault source, and this build has the evidence.** The
   blocker is contamination at exactly that node. Every awkward, hot, flux-heavy attempt there is a
   chance to deposit the thing we are trying to remove.

**Use 40 AWG magnet wire** — Berard's choice, already in `docs/BOM.md` §7, limp enough not to load
the scanner. Keep it short, leave a service loop rather than pulling it taut, solder the preamp end
first, clean the flux, and let it touch nothing on the way.

**Sequencing: take the open-input measurement BEFORE rebuilding the lead.** The cut coax is
precisely what makes that bisection test possible, and rebuilding first throws it away.

This closes a `CHOICE` that has been open since the BOM was written, and it closes it **toward
upstream** — Berard was right, for a reason he never stated.

## 20. New faults

### 20.1 The ADC's differential reference is floating — THE MAJOR FINDING

The LTC2326-16 measures **`PREAMP+` minus `PREAMP-`**. It does not measure the preamp output
against ground. `PREAMP-` is meant to carry the preamp's own ground back on its own wire, which is
Berard's method for rejecting noise picked up in the cable.

**It is connected to nothing.**

> **Every ADC number in this project has been measured against an undefined, drifting reference.**

**This is the same fault as `STATUS.md` fault 5**, recorded on 2026-08-31 as *"two pins wander
when only one should"* and filed as a minor loose end awaiting a rebuild. The two wandering pins
are the output (legitimately at 11.9 V) and this floating ground return.

**It is repairable without a rebuild.** The gerber netlist confirms JP1 pins 1 and 4 are both GND
on the board, so bonding the open one to the good one is electrically correct — the old
"do not run a wire between JP1 pins" rule was retired on 2026-09-06 for exactly this reason. The
break is in one of three places: a solder joint at JP1, the cable, or the DSUB2 end. **Possibly a
one-wire fix.**

### 20.2 The ADC is being driven roughly 3x past its input span

11.9 V presented to a converter whose span is +/-4.096 V, with gain-1 buffers and **nothing in the
path limiting it.**

`docs/UPSTREAM_MECHPANDA.md` §5 already warned:

> *"U21 runs on +/-15 V while the ADC runs on 5 V and 3.3 V. The buffer can therefore present far
> more than the ADC's +/-4.096 V input span if the preamp output ever swings hard. Nothing in
> between limits it. VERIFY the LTC2326-16's absolute-maximum input against the datasheet before
> deliberately driving the preamp to its rails."*

**Nobody verified it, and the preamp has apparently been sitting at 11.9 V since at least
31 August.** Whether this has damaged the converter is unknown.

### 20.3 The preamp output may be saturated

+11.905 V on a +15 V supply is close to an OPA627's output swing limit. **If it is pinned, it
cannot respond to anything**, which would make today's three null results artefacts rather than
evidence. Not established either way — see §22.

### 20.4 The board takes over 45 minutes to settle

See §19.2. Newly discovered and previously uncontrolled for.

## 28. The rule, now in `CLAUDE.md` §3c

**Before any instruction that changes a connection, a supply, or sends a command: compute the
resulting state at every node it touches. If a part could be taken past a limit, get the limit
first — the repository, then the web. If the number cannot be got, say so and do not order the
action.** Never sequence a protective check after the risky step. Always state the expected
reading.

**"Check the datasheet" is not an instruction to hand to Jacob and Nuh. It is work that was not
done.**

## 29. Doing that work found a larger error: the ADC full scale is 10.24 V

The LTC2326-16 datasheet, read 2026-09-07:

| | |
|---|---|
| Analog input range | **±10.24 V true bipolar** |
| REFBUF | **4.096 V** — an onboard buffer gains the 2.048 V bandgap by 2 |
| Relationship | **input span = 2.5 × REFBUF** |
| Output format | **two's complement** |
| Input type | **pseudo-differential** — `PREAMP-` is not a wide-range input |

**`_ref_buffer_volts = 4.096f` in the driver is the REFBUF voltage, exactly as its name says.**
Not the input span.

**`docs/UPSTREAM_MECHPANDA.md` §1 argued for 4.096 V, and its own closing paragraph named the
missing step — "confirm from the datasheet how the full-scale input range relates to REFBUF".
That step was never taken. The question was closed on inference and the wrong answer was then
pushed through `STATUS.md`, `docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md`, `docs/INDEX.md`,
`docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` and `Code/pc/README.md`.**

**`stm_control.py` and `stm_console.py` were right the whole time and must not be changed.**

### What it changes

| | Was recorded | Actually |
|---|---|---|
| Volts per count | 0.125 mV | **0.3125 mV** |
| 1 nA in counts | 800 | **320** |
| Max measurable current | 40.96 nA | **102.4 nA** |

The contradiction was: 11.9 V on a ±4.096 V input should pin at 32767, but it reads ~30,000 and

At **0.3125 mV per count**, 29,500 counts is **9.22 V**. PAD1 measures 11.905 V. So `PREAMP-` sits

**Nothing is saturated. Nothing is over range. The ADC is very probably undamaged.** The "about

## 33. What the datasheets changed

### 33.1 The DAC floating-pin hypothesis is very likely dead

**`RESET` on the AD5761R has an internal pull-up and may be left floating. So does `LDAC`.** [DS]

`STATUS.md` fault 4's leading hypothesis — "CLEAR#/RESET# float and glitch together" — **required
those pins to be genuinely undriven.** They are internally pulled up. The 20 unconnected pads found
in the netlist are **by design and the datasheet permits them.**

**And the correct explanation was already in the repository.** `docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF_SUMMARY.md`
lines 476–495, under "THE OPERATING RULE THAT MATTERS MOST", explains the symptom completely as
**power sequencing**: USB boots the Teensy in milliseconds, `setup()` writes DAC configuration into
chips whose analog supply is not up yet, and the writes go nowhere. It even explains the one time it
appeared to fix itself — a reflash reboots the Teensy *after* the rails are up.

**Nuh's data today fits it exactly**: LED1–4 lit at **every** power-on without exception, cleared by
`RSET`, and **not** triggered by 30 minutes of idle.

> **Consequence: the proposed two-wire fix onto ribbon pins 24/26 is very likely unnecessary.**
> Nobody should solder for this. The fix is operational and already written down.
>
> This is the same pattern as the JP1 pinout: the handoff had it, the handoff is ranked last, and
> it was skipped. **That is now the seventh instance.**

### 33.2 `PREAMP−` floating is a specification violation, not just an inconvenience

**The LTC2326-16 is pseudo-differential and requires IN− to stay within ±500 mV of GND.** [DS]

Ours sits at about **2.7 V** — **five times outside the allowed range.**

Today's log said the readings were "measured against an undefined, drifting node". That understates
it. **The converter has been operated outside its specified input conditions for the entire life of
the project.** No ADC number from before the bond can be rehabilitated by rescaling.

### 33.3 The absolute maximum question, answered well enough to act

**IN+/IN− absolute maximum is −16.5 V to +16.5 V** — **[DS-SIB]**, from the sibling LTC2326-18,
because every host carrying the -16 datasheet is blocked from this session.

**So bonding `PREAMP−` while PAD1 sits at 11.9 V is very likely safe**, and it *fixes* the spec
violation above. The reading will **pin at 32767**, which is correct behaviour, not damage.
**Still marked UNVERIFIED for our exact part**, and the plan is arranged so it never gates anything.

## 34. What the second pass found in today's own work

### 34.1 The noise does not add up, and the explanation is probably the reference

Computed against the datasheets for the first time:

| Source | Contribution at the output |
|---|---|
| Johnson noise of the 100 MΩ, 12.9 fA/√Hz over ~8 kHz | **~0.12 mV RMS** [CALC] |
| OPA627 voltage noise × noise gain 67, over ~5 kHz | **~0.02 mV RMS** [CALC] |
| **Measured 2026-08-31** — 623 counts at the corrected 0.3125 mV | **195 mV RMS** [MEAS] |

**About 1,700× the resistor's thermal floor and roughly 10,000× the op-amp's own contribution.
Neither device explains it.**

> **New hypothesis: the "noise" is the floating `PREAMP−` wandering, not the preamp.** A node with
> no defined impedance, sitting 2.7 V outside the converter's allowed range, produces exactly this.
> It also fits the hour-long warm-up.
>
> **If confirmed, every noise figure in this project is void** — including today's observation that
> the shield "nearly halved the noise, 780 pA to 429 pA". Those numbers were already flagged as
> unattributable; they may not be measurements of the preamp at all.
>
> **Test M2 in the plan settles it and costs two minutes:** watch PAD1 on a meter while the ADC
> scatters.

### 34.2 The TIA is stable only by accident, and its bandwidth is 5 kHz not 103 kHz

Stability needs `C_f ≥ √(C_in / (2π · R_f · GBW))`. With R_f = 100 MΩ, GBW = 16 MHz [DS] and
C_in ≈ 20 pF, that is **C_f ≥ 0.045 pF**. [CALC]

**No compensation capacitor is fitted.** Stability rests entirely on the feedback resistor's own
stray capacitance, typically 0.1–0.5 pF. It has margin, but it is unintentional margin.

**Practical consequence: do not shorten the feedback resistor's leads or lay it flat on the board
during the rebuild.** Air-mounting keeps the stray predictable — and keeps the input node off the
board surface, which is the other reason it is done that way.

**And the bandwidth this implies is ~5 kHz**, from `1/(2π·R_f·C_f)` at 0.3 pF. **The TIA is the
bandwidth limit of the whole current chain, not the 103 kHz anti-alias filter.** Nothing in the
repository had computed that.

### 34.3 119 nA against an OPA627 rules the op-amp out

The OPA627's own input bias current is **~1 pA** [DS]. Our measured input current is **119 nA** —
**119,000 times** the device's own contribution. [CALC]

**A damaged op-amp was never on the candidate list and can now be ruled out without testing.**
Nothing about the part explains the number. The leakage is external: surface conduction on the
board, at the PTFE standoff, or through the enclosure.

### 34.4 Teensy voltage compatibility — audited end to end, and it is clean

**Teensy 4.1 GPIO are 3.3 V and NOT 5 V tolerant; driving any pin above 3.3 V causes permanent
damage.** [DS]

Traced every signal that reaches the Teensy:
- DAC `VLOGIC` and the ADC's I/O supply are both **3.3 V** from U16.
- **`ADC_SDO` and `ADC_BUSY` are the only board → Teensy signals**, both from U15's 3.3 V rail.
- The ULN2003's outputs go to the motor, never back to the Teensy.
- **No 5 V or ±15 V node has a path to a Teensy pin anywhere in the netlist. Verified.**

> **The one way to break this is miswiring the motor driver** — the ULN2003 module's 5 V pin sits
> next to its inputs. **Meter before connecting.**

### 34.5 Three things that look like bugs and are not — do not "fix" them

| Looks wrong | Actually |
|---|---|
| `EfficientStepper(..., IN1, IN3, IN2, IN4)` | **Deliberate.** 1-3-2-4 is the 28BYJ-48's coil sequence |
| `LTC2326_16::read()` drives CS **high** to start | The pin is `RDL/SDI`, not a conventional CS. The handoff says explicitly **"Do not change the RDL polarity. It is correct as written."** |
| `SPI_MODE2` on the DAC bus | **Correct.** The AD5761R clocks data in on the **falling** edge, and MODE2 (CPOL 1, CPHA 0) samples there [DS] |

### 34.6 Two component purposes nobody had recorded

- **R6, R9, R14, R19 (220 Ω into DSUB1) are stability isolation resistors, not just protection.**
  An op-amp driving a ~15 nF piezo directly is a classic oscillator; a series resistor is the
  standard cure. RC = 3.3 µs → 48 kHz, far above any scan rate. [CALC]
- **The 1 MHz SPI limit is the ribbon, confirmed.** The AD5761R accepts **50 MHz** [DS]. The
  constraint was never the chip.

### 34.7 A new minor risk: `TONE` may current-limit the output stage

`TONE` puts a square wave on Z. A 10 V edge through the 220 Ω series resistor demands
**~45 mA peak**, which likely exceeds the OPA2227's output current capability. It will not damage
anything — the part limits — but **the edges will be current-limited, so `TONE` does not deliver
the waveform it appears to.** `CLAUDE.md` already says not to judge the piezo by ear; this is the
electrical reason why. **[UNVERIFIED]** against the OPA2227 output-current spec.

## 36. Process lesson

**Two of today's largest findings came from datasheets that were never opened, and a third from a
document ranked last in this repository's own precedence order.** The ADC full scale, the DAC
pull-ups and the power-sequencing explanation were all available before any measurement was taken.

`CLAUDE.md` §3b already says to search the repository before recording something as unknown.
**§3c, added today, extends it: get the number before ordering the test.** The pattern in both is
the same — **the cheapest source was skipped because inference felt sufficient.**
