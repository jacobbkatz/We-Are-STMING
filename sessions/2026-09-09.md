## 4. New faults

**One, in the design we fabricated from, not in anything we did.**

**C2 is reverse-connected.** `C2` is a `CPOL-US` polarized tantalum, 4.7 µF, case C. The netlist puts
its **anode on the −15 V rail and its cathode on ground**. Ground is the more positive node, so as
drawn the part is reverse-biased by the full rail voltage. C1, on the positive rail, is correct.

A tantalum's reverse rating is a small fraction of its forward rating — typically quoted around 10%,
often capped near 1 V. At 15 V reverse it leaks, self-heats, and this is the classic tantalum
short-and-ignite failure.

**Verified in two independent files that agree:** the Eagle schematic netlist and the gerber X2 pin
attributes (`C2,+` flashes on the `-9V` net at X 9.7575, `C2,-` on `GND` at X 15.0075).

**UNKNOWN: how C2 is actually fitted on our physical board.** Nobody has looked. **Check before
applying power** — buzz each C2 terminal to JP1 pin 1; on a tantalum the printed stripe marks the
**anode**, so the striped end should be the one that beeps to ground.

## 6. False trails

**Three, all mine, all worth recording because two of them nearly went into the repository as fact.**

### 6.1 An op-amp identification read off someone else's board

A photograph was shared showing a `tunnelAmp` board with the marking `A BB OPA124U`. On the strength
of it, `docs/FACTS.md`, `docs/BOM.md`, `docs/COMPONENTS.md`, `docs/UPSTREAM_BERARD.md` and
`docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF_SUMMARY.md` were all edited to say the fitted part is an OPA124U, and the TIA
stability analysis was recomputed at 1.5 MHz gain-bandwidth.

**The photograph was Dan Berard's board, not ours.** Every one of those edits was reversed.

**What survives** is a genuine open question, not a correction: `docs/BOM.md` says OPA627AU and
"CONFIRMED"; the Eagle source says `OPA124U`. Those disagree and **nobody has read our own package
marking**. Weight of evidence favours OPA124U — three independent sources say it and the lone
OPA627AU is one repository line whose basis is unknown — but that is inference, and this project
does not overturn a CONFIRMED record on inference. Logged in `docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md`.

It changes the stability margin (~7x for an OPA627, ~2x for an OPA124) and **nothing about the
119 nA**, since both parts have ~1 pA bias current.

### 6.2 A dimension taken from a search snippet

The Keystone 11301's mounting hole was first recorded as **Ø2.03 mm** from a search result summary,
with the conclusion that it would drop into our Ø2.108 mm hole. **2.03 mm is the part's *length below
the flange*, not a diameter.** The real figure is **Ø2.184 mm**, which is *larger* than our hole —
the fit is interference, not clearance, and that changes what happens at the bench. Corrected once
the manufacturer's own parameter table was available.

### 6.3 PETG-CF conductivity — a constraint, not a lead

Carbon-fibre-filled filament is **static-dissipative**, roughly 10⁴–10¹¹ Ω/sq against ~10¹⁵ for
unfilled polymer, and the box standoffs touch the board. Worth knowing, and it is a reason not to
print box v2 with a boss under the input node.

**But it cannot explain the 119 nA offset.** A dissipative path from the input node to a *grounded*
box carries no DC current — the input node sits at virtual ground and the box sits at ground.
`docs/UPSTREAM_BERARD.md` §4 already makes this argument about the coax shield. **Not worth bench
time.**

## 9. Process lessons

**The repository answered every question asked of it.** The drill file, the box mesh, the Eagle
source and the gerber X2 attributes between them settled the box fit, the missing component values,
the guard-ring question and the C2 defect. None of it needed a bench. `CLAUDE.md` §3b is holding up.

**But two findings were nearly manufactured out of nothing:**

1. **A photograph is not a measurement of *our* hardware.** `CLAUDE.md` §7 says not to read wire
   colours off a photo. The reason generalises to any hardware fact, and the rule should probably say
   so. **Ask whose board it is before reading anything off it.**
2. **A search-result summary is not a datasheet.** The 11301's "2.03 mm" was a different dimension
   entirely. When a distributor's parameter table finally became available it flipped the conclusion
   from clearance to interference.

**And one about the checker.** `Code/pc/check_facts.py` narrows a retired value to lines containing
its qualifying words. The first `5.93 mm` entry was qualified as *"the preamp mounting-hole spacing"*
— wording that appears in no document — so **the checker reported clean while three live files still
carried the wrong number**. Broadening the qualifier to *"the mounting holes"* made it catch all of
them at once.

> **A silent checker is worse than no checker, because it is believed.** When adding a RETIRED row,
> phrase the qualifier in the words the documents actually use, then confirm it fires before
> trusting it.
