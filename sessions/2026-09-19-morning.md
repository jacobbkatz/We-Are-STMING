## 4. What the day establishes, and what it does not

**Established, measured:**
1. **The junction is not a clean tunnelling gap at any bias tried**: shallow, and hysteretic by 705-1,868
   counts (medians) in every run (§3.10-3.11).
2. **The gap moves by most of the Z range within seconds to minutes**, with the motor and hands still
   (≥ 43,000 counts in 6.4 s; ≥ 56,000 over ~2 min) (§3.9, 3.12).
3. **After every Z-0 back-off the gold was out of reach at the first full-range check** (§3.4, 3.8, 3.14).
4. **Neither 20-step motor find gave a usable gap; the motor did not release a hard contact that the hand did
   release** (§3.3, 3.7, 3.8, 3.12).
5. **A big upward Z jump can put a false contact on the next read** (§3.5).

**NOT established — each a live hypothesis for the break:**
- **What moves the gold. Four untested candidates, unranked:** the leaf on its paper (compliant, follows the
  tip); the plate on its rubber bands and ball contacts (creep after every push); thermal motion of the
  printed head; **air currents** (the bag was on only while the gold was out of reach). Plate on all three
  balls (`SAID`).
- **The Z scale.** A decade per ~1,800 counts would be tunnelling only if Z moved the tip ~140-290x less
  than the inherited 0.016 nm/count. The hysteresis argues against a clean gap; the scale is still unmeasured.
- **Hand overshoot against drift after release.** Not separated by any measurement.
- **Thread play in the side screws** (proposed at ~11:5x). The touch test found no effect of a still hand.
