## 6. False trails, and things ruled out

### 6.1 C2 polarity is not the 119 nA. Ruled out by argument, not by measurement.

`N$4` has **exactly one connection in the entire design** — `IC1.-IN`. One pad, no track, no via.
C2 connects the −15 V net to GND and touches neither.

**And C2's two pads sit at −15 V and 0 V whichever way the part inside is facing.** The copper,
the soldermask and any contamination on them see an identical voltage map either way, so the
surface-leakage path toward the input pad is unchanged. The only thing polarity alters is leakage
*inside* the capacitor, anode to cathode, which flows −15 V to GND entirely within the supply loop.

**Nor the other symptoms:**

| Symptom | Why C2 does not explain it |
|---|---|
| 45-minute warm-up drift | C2 sits **7.1 mm** from IC1. A 6032 part on a 20 × 15 mm board settles thermally in a minute or two, not 45. Raising it 1 °C needs roughly 10 mW — about **0.7 mA** of leakage, obvious on the bench supply |
| Noise | OPA627 PSRR is over 100 dB, and the recorded noise is probably the floating `PREAMP−` |
| Anything at all | The −15 V rail measured **−15.237 V** on 2026-09-07. A significantly leaking C2 would load it |

**Do not re-rank the 119 nA candidates because of C2.** The superglue at the PTFE standoff is
unaffected and remains the lead.

### 6.2 Continuity testing cannot detect capacitor polarity

Jacob recalled testing continuity at C2. **A capacitor is a DC open in both directions**, and
buzzing its terminals identifies which *net* each pad is on — which is the same either way. Only
reading the stripe under magnification settles it. The earlier test was not wrong, it was the
wrong instrument for this question.

### 6.3 An inference from document structure, presented as a finding

`docs/BOM.md` §3 lists preamp parts individually and `gerbers/README.md` prices only *controller*
assembly, so this session concluded the preamp board was hand-populated. **It was JLCPCB PCBA.**
See §8.

## 10. Piezo solder — which one to buy, added later the same day

**Jacob asked which solder to buy, specifically a syringe rather than a jar.** Documentation only,
nothing powered, nothing measured.

### 10.1 There are two solders in this project and they are not interchangeable

This took a moment to untangle because the repository says both "do not buy solder" and "buy one
syringe of solder paste", and both are correct — they are about different joints.

| Job | Alloy | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Everything electrical** — JP1, the turret, the tip lead, the shield seams | Sn99.3/Ag0.3/Cu0.7 lead-free wire | **We own it. Do not buy more.** `docs/INVENTORY.md` |
| **The four piezo quadrant wires, and only those** | **Sn42/Bi58 low-temp paste** | `docs/BOM.md` §5 asks for one syringe. **Not in `INVENTORY.md` — nobody has said whether we own any** |

**The syringe Jacob was asking about is the second one.** It is the only paste in the build.

### 10.2 The apparent contradiction about bismuth, resolved

`sessions/2026-08-31-results.md` rule 4 says **"do not use bismuth low-temp solder"**, and
`docs/PREAMP_SHOPPING_LIST.md` repeated it in its "Do not buy" line without a scope. Read cold,
that flatly contradicts `docs/BOM.md` §5.

**Both are right. The 08-31 rule is about the preamp board, where the list it sits in is titled
"Rules for the rebuild".** Its two reasons were that bismuth is brittle, and that bismuth meeting
leaded solder forms a eutectic near 96 C. Neither reason reaches the piezo:

- **Brittleness.** A quadrant wire is a 40 AWG hair glued flat to a disc that never sees mechanical
  load. Brittleness matters at a connector or a board that flexes, not here.
- **The 96 C eutectic.** That needs lead. **We own no leaded solder** — ours is Sn99.3/Ag0.3/Cu0.7.
  The hazard cannot arise in this workshop as currently stocked. **If leaded solder is ever bought,
  this reactivates**, which is why it is now a row in `docs/FACTS.md` rather than a line in one
  session log.

Both files have been scoped so the next person does not have to re-derive this.

### 10.3 The product

**Chip Quik `SMDLTLFPT4`** — Sn42/Bi57.6/Ag0.4, 138 C, **5 cc / 15 g syringe**, T4 powder.

Chosen over the unbranded 30-50 g listings for three reasons:

1. **It has a published datasheet with a flux classification (REL0) and a stated shelf life.** The
   unbranded ones state neither. On a build whose blocking fault is surface contamination, an
   unspecified flux is not a small unknown.
2. **The Ag0.4 addition answers the brittleness objection directly.** Micro-alloying silver into
   Sn-Bi is the standard industrial fix for exactly the failure mode 08-31 raised. Same 138 C.
3. **15 g in a 5 cc syringe is the small one.** Four joints need milligrams. **Shelf life, not
   quantity, is the binding constraint**, so a bigger tub is worse, not better value.

**Amazon and chipquik.com were both blocked by the network egress proxy, so the price and stock
could not be confirmed.** DigiKey lists it as part 8543525. **VERIFY the price before ordering** —
a third-party figure of about $22 was the only number visible and it is not a source to trust.

### 10.4 The shelf-life trap, which changes when to buy

Chip Quik's own figures: **refrigerated at 3-8 C, better than 6 months; unrefrigerated, better than
2 months.** It needs 4 hours to come back to room temperature before use.

**The preamp is the blocker and the piezo work is not next.** Ordering this today and leaving it in
a drawer means it is likely out of life before it is opened. Either buy it with the piezo discs when
that work actually starts, or buy it now and **put it in the fridge the day it arrives.** Not the
freezer.

### 10.5 The iron temperature — an open question since 2026-09-05, now largely closed

`docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` §4 has carried "actual iron temperature and dwell for our discs" as
undocumented. **It was answerable from arithmetic already in the repository.**

The tip is the only heat source, so nothing in the assembly can end up hotter than the tip. **Set
the station below the depolarisation ceiling and the ceramic cannot reach it however long the iron
is held there.** That removes dwell from the problem entirely and leaves one dial setting. The
melt is 138 C, the ceiling about 210 C: **set 190 C.**

**What is still VERIFY, and it is the whole remaining risk:** displayed temperature is not tip
temperature, and we own no tip thermometer. A station reading 20 C low sits exactly on the ceiling.
**The mitigation is a sacrificial disc** — which is why `docs/BOM.md` §5 already says buy five or
more. **A ruined disc still reads correctly on a meter**, so that test has to be mechanical, not a
resistance check.

Full derivation and its caveats are in `docs/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` §4. Values are in `docs/FACTS.md`.

**This also replaces "low temperature and short dwell" as the stated technique.** Short dwell was a
proxy for keeping the ceramic cool, and it is a poor one — it puts a beginner under time pressure at
a delicate joint. **Set the dial right and take your time instead.**

### 10.6 The flux on this paste is the no-clean type we just rejected — and it still matters here

Chip Quik's low-temp pastes carry a **synthetic no-clean flux**, which is precisely the chemistry
`sessions/2026-09-09.md` §10.2 ruled out for the preamp because its residue does not fully dissolve
in IPA. **No rosin-fluxed Sn42/Bi58 paste could be found; that is recorded as not-found, not as
does-not-exist.**

**Why it is acceptable on the piezo and where the actual risk sits.** The quadrant wires are driven
by a low-impedance amplifier output — 100 MOhm of leakage there is nothing. But the disc is where
the **tip holder** mounts, on a sapphire insulator, and the tip is the input node. **100 MOhm from a
quadrant at 10 V across to the tip injects 100 nA** — the same order as the 119 nA already blocking
this build, and roughly a hundred times a tunneling current.

**So the mitigation is build order, not chemistry:** solder and clean all four quadrant wires
first, then build the sapphire, tip holder and tip on top of a board that is already clean. **Never
the other way round.** Use the least paste that will make the joint.

## 11. The flux, in a syringe rather than a jar — added later the same day

**Jacob's actual question**, which section 10 answered wrongly: not the piezo solder, **the flux for
the preamp rebuild**, and whether it comes in a syringe rather than a jar. Documentation only.

### 11.1 The answer is no, and that is a finding rather than a dead end

**Chip Quik's RMA791 is jar-only.** Its whole paste-flux family — RA691, RMA771, RMA791, RA891 — is
2 oz flat jars. No syringe SKU exists. **`docs/PREAMP_SHOPPING_LIST.md` said "50 g jar" because
there was no alternative, not because a jar was preferred**, and its own item line still asked for a
"gel syringe". Those two disagreed and nobody had noticed.

### 11.2 Why the obvious substitution is not safe

Chip Quik's syringe products are a **different line — "tack flux"** — and their J-STD-004 codes and
their own vendor descriptions **contradict each other about the chemistry.**

In J-STD-004 the first two letters are the chemistry: **`RO` is rosin, `RE` is synthetic resin.** So
the code should settle our one criterion outright. **It does not.**

- **SMD491-10M** is classified **ROL0** — and TestEquity's own product title is "Tack Flux,
  **Synthetic** No-Clean, Halogen-Free, ROL0."
- **NC191** is **ROL0** and is described in the same breath as a "synthetic no-clean flux".
- **RMA591NL** is **ROM1**, has "RMA" in its name, and is described as "low viscosity RMA
  **synthetic** paste".
- **SMD291**, the most widely stocked of them, is **REL0** — **`RE`, so not rosin at all** — despite
  a composition list that mentions rosin.

**This is the same trap that produced the flux we already own**, which is a non-rosin no-clean.
Buying a syringe off the code alone would repeat it.

### 11.3 What could not be verified, and why that is the whole point

**chipquik.com, mouser.com and amazon.com are all blocked by this session's network egress proxy.
Not one primary datasheet could be opened.** Every classification above comes from a search snippet.

**So no recommendation was made among those four.** `CLAUDE.md` §3c: if the number cannot be got,
say so and give a route that does not depend on it.

### 11.4 The route that does not depend on it

**Keep the RMA791 jar. Buy empty 10 cc Luer-lock syringes and load them from it.** About $10 for
ten. The chemistry stays the one already reasoned through — **ROL0: rosin, low activity,
halide-free**, the mildest of everything surveyed. Back-load the barrel with the plunger removed;
paste will not draw up through a tip.

**A better argument for the syringe than the one already written down.** The shopping list justified
a syringe only as "less awkward for surface-mount rework". The real reason on this board is
different: **an open jar that a brush is repeatedly dipped into becomes a contamination reservoir** —
skin oils, board debris, spent flux, all returned to the supply. Given that a human body within a
metre injects 20-50 nA into this input node, re-dipping into a shared jar is not a small thing. A
syringe dispenses a fresh bead and the bulk is never touched.

**Single-purchase alternative, with its cost stated:** **MG Chemicals 8342**, 10 mL / 9.5 g syringe.
The only syringe flux found whose **manufacturer's product name** says rosin — "RA Rosin Flux Paste"
— rather than only a code. Its TDS says residue removal is **required** and that IPA removes it,
which matches our protocol better than any no-clean does. **But it is RA, not RMA:** ROM1, with
**0.5-2.0% halides by weight.** §10.2 of Nuh's log chose RMA over RA specifically because the milder
activator fails gracefully if a trace survives. **Halides do not fail gracefully**, so with this one
the distilled-water rinse becomes the step that carries the risk rather than a refinement.

### 11.5 Still open

**The flux pen.** The shopping list asked for "gel syringe **and a pen**" and only the paste was ever
chosen. **Most flux pens are low-solids no-clean — the wrong chemistry.** No rosin pen has been
verified. Recorded rather than guessed.

### 11.6 Process note

**Section 10 of this log answers a question that was not asked.** "Solder" was read literally when
the context — a rebuild whose entire consumables list is about flux and cleaning — pointed at the
flux. The section is kept because its content is independently correct and it closed a real open
question, but **the misread cost a round trip.** Where a request is ambiguous and the repository
makes one reading far more likely, say which reading is being taken before doing the work.
