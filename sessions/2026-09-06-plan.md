Jacob is away. This is written so you can run the whole thing without calling him.

## Before you start: the rules that cost hardware

1. **LED1–LED4 must be DARK.** Look at them immediately before and immediately after every single
   measurement. If any is lit, the DACs have lost configuration and **the reading is void** —
   send `RSET`, then `DACZ 32768`, and take it again.
2. **Do not run `APRH`.** Ever, today.
3. **Do not send `CCON`** with a tip anywhere near the sample. It snaps Z to midscale — up to a
   180 nm lurch.
4. **`DACZ 32768` after every `RSET` and every `TEST`.** Both leave Z at a rail.
5. **Nobody within a metre of the preamp** during any preamp measurement. A person injects
   20–50 nA; a tunneling current is about 1 nA.
6. **No cyanoacrylate** near the preamp or in its box.

# BLOCK A — power off, meter only (about 30 min)

**Board unpowered for all of Block A.** Nothing here can damage anything.

## A1. The DAC reset pins — the most valuable test today

**Why:** the schematic marks CLEAR# (pin 2) and RESET# (pin 3) as *not connected* on all four
DACs. If they really are floating, electrical noise can trigger a reset — and four floating pins in
the same box would trigger **together**, which is exactly your "all four DACs die at once" fault.

**Do this:** with the board off, put the meter on continuity. U1 is the leftmost DAC in the row of
four across the middle of the board (see `docs/WIRING.md` §9 for the layout). Pin 1 is marked with
a dot on the package.

Measure from **U1 pin 2** and **U1 pin 3** to any 3.3 V point on the board.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Open (no beep)** | **The pins are floating. This is very likely the cause of the DAC fault.** |
| Beeps / low resistance | They are tied high, and this hypothesis is wrong |

Repeat for U2, U3, U4 if U1 is open.

**Write down what you find. Do not solder anything yet** — Jacob and Claude should confirm the
datasheet question first (whether the chip has internal pull-ups). If it is open, the fix is four
short wires to 3.3 V and it is permanent, not a workaround.

## A2. The preamp shield

**Why:** the shielding was never grounded, and an ungrounded shield can be worse than none.

Open the preamp box. With the meter:

| Check | What to record |
|---|---|
| Continuity between opposite corners of the shield tape | Does the shield connect to itself at all? Aluminium tape adhesive is often non-conductive, so overlapping strips may not connect |
| Do the copper tape and aluminium tape touch anywhere? | Look and probe. In contact with humidity they form a small battery |
| Continuity from the shield to circuit ground | Expect **open** — that is the fault we are fixing |

## A2b. Identify the JP1 pins — newly safe to do

**Why:** since 2026-08-31 this has been marked "do not touch", because if the pin numbering were
mirrored you could bond what you thought was ground to −15 V and short a rail. **The numbering is
now known** — it was extracted from the preamp gerbers on 2026-09-06.

| Pin | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net | GND | **+15 V** | **OUTPUT** | GND | **−15 V** |

**The orientation rule** (the layout is asymmetric, so this cannot be got backwards):

> **The negative supply is at the very END of the row. The positive supply is one in from the
> OTHER end.** The middle pin is always the output.

**Do this**, with the preamp powered from the controller and the board on the bench:

1. Measure each of the five pins against controller ground.
2. Find the two supply pins. **The one at an end is pin 5.** Work back from the other end: pin 1,
   pin 2 (+15 V), pin 3 (output), pin 4, pin 5 (−15 V).
3. Pins **1 and 4** should both read a solid **0.000 V**.

On 2026-08-31 one of them wandered instead. **Now you can say which one.**

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Both pin 1 and pin 4 read 0.000 V | The earlier finding has gone away. Record it |
| One of them wanders | **Record which.** That ground is open |

**You may now fix it** if you want: pins 1 and 4 are both GND on the board, so bonding the open one
to the good one at the header is electrically correct. The old warning no longer applies. **But
record the measurement first**, before changing anything.

## A3. Tip holder isolation

**Why:** Berard warns that glue must not bridge the tip standoff to the grounded brass piezo plate.
That path costs signal and adds noise.

Measure between the **tip holder** and the **brass piezo plate**.

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Open | Correct |
| Beeps | There is a short. Record it — do not try to fix it today |

# BLOCK C — during the 30 minutes: prepare the shield fix (30 min)

**Do not send any commands to the board while the timer runs.**

Ground the preamp case shield:

1. **Solder the ground wire to the copper tape**, not the aluminium — aluminium's oxide layer will
   not take solder.
2. **Bond at ONE point only.** More than one creates a ground loop and trades one noise problem for
   another.
3. Connect it to circuit ground at the preamp.
4. If copper and aluminium tape are touching anywhere (from A2), separate them if you can do it
   without disturbing anything else.

**Do not close the box yet** — Block D may want to change things.

# BLOCK D — the preamp, the actual blocker (about 60 min)

This is the important block. **Bench clear, nobody within a metre, for every capture.**

## D1. Baseline, shield NOT yet connected

Disconnect the shield wire you just made, so this matches the old measurements.

```bash
python Code/pc/adc_stats.py -n 50 -i 9.0 --tag "before shield, undisturbed"
```

That takes about 8 minutes. **Walk away while it runs.** Check LED1–4 before and after.

**The number to beat: it settled at 29873 counts on 2026-08-31.**

## D2. The rail-scaling test — new, and it discriminates between two causes

**Why:** if the offset comes from a supply rail leaking into the input across a dirty surface, it
should **shrink when you lower the rails**. If it comes from the shield or something else, it will
not move.

1. Note the offset from D1.
2. Turn the bench supply down from **±18 V to about ±13 V** input.
   (The regulators need headroom, so do not go below about ±17 V if you want the ±15 V rails
   intact — **instead**, if your supply allows, measure the actual ±15 V rails and reduce until
   they read about ±10 V. If the regulators drop out the rails go unstable and the test is void.)
3. Re-run a short capture:

```bash
python Code/pc/adc_stats.py -n 20 -i 5.0 --tag "rails reduced"
```

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Offset drops roughly in proportion to the rail voltage | **The leakage path is from a supply rail to the input.** Contamination is the cause; the rebuild is the fix |
| Offset barely moves | The rails are not the source. The shield or something else is |

**Put the rails back to ±18 V afterwards** and confirm LED5 and LED6 are lit.

## D3. Connect the shield and re-measure

Connect the shield ground wire. Same conditions as D1.

```bash
python Code/pc/adc_stats.py -n 50 -i 9.0 --tag "shield grounded"
```

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Settles near 0 counts** | **Fixed.** The shield was the whole problem. Stage 6 passes |
| Settles at a few thousand | Large improvement, some leakage left. Usable |
| **Still near 29873** | The shield was not the cause. The board rebuild is next |
| Ramps to a rail | Something got disconnected — check your work |

**Whatever happens, you have learned something and you have not consumed the spare board.**

# BLOCK E — motor direction (about 20 min)

**Why:** `stm_approach.py` refuses to run without knowing which way the motor advances the tip. It
is a required safety answer.

**Tip removed, or the head backed well away from the sample.**

```bash
python Code/pc/stm_console.py DACZ 32768
python Code/pc/stm_console.py MTMV 500
```

500 steps takes about 7 seconds and moves the screw roughly 77 µm — visible if you watch the screw
or put a finger lightly on the plate.

**Watch which way the scan head plate moves: toward the sample, or away.**

| Result | Record as |
|---|---|
| Positive `MTMV` moves the tip **toward** the sample | `--motor-toward-sample positive` |
| Positive `MTMV` moves the tip **away** | `--motor-toward-sample negative` |

Then send `MTMV -500` to put it back.

**Also, immediately after:** touch the motor body. **Is it warm?** Our firmware never de-energises
it, and Berard reports that heating drifts a scanner out of range within minutes. Record yes or no.

# BLOCK F — the dummy junction test (about 30 min)

**Only if Block D improved the offset.** If the preamp still sits near 29873 counts there is no
headroom for this and it will just saturate — skip to Block G.

**Why this is worth doing:** it proves the entire current path end to end **with no tip installed
and no crash risk**, it gives counts-per-amp directly, and it **tells you which way the tunneling
current moves the reading**. That last one is on the open-questions list and nothing else answers
it without risking a tip.

**What to use:** a **100 MOhm resistor, or larger.** Do NOT use 1 MOhm.

> The 2026-08-31 plan says "a resistor between 1 MOhm and 100 MOhm". **The low end of that range is
> wrong.** The preamp can measure to about 100 nA. At 1 MOhm with 3 V of bias you would push 3 uA
> through it — thirty times over range, instantly saturated. At 100 MOhm you get about 30 nA, which
> is comfortably in range. Corrected here.

**Do this:**

1. **No tip installed.** Clip the resistor between the **sample holder** and the **tip holder**.
2. Check LED1–LED4 are dark.
3. Set the bias to several values and record the ADC reading at each:

```bash
python Code/pc/stm_console.py BIAS 32768
python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR
python Code/pc/stm_console.py BIAS 40000
python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR
python Code/pc/stm_console.py BIAS 50000
python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR
python Code/pc/stm_console.py BIAS 25000
python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR
python Code/pc/stm_console.py BIAS 15000
python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR
```

4. Put the bias back: `BIAS 32768`.

**Write down all five pairs.** What they give you:

| From the data | What it settles |
|---|---|
| A straight line through the points | The whole current path works, end to end |
| The **slope** | **Counts per amp**, directly measured. Better than the R23 method, and it settles the 4.096 versus 10.24 question outright |
| The **direction** — does more positive bias push the reading up or down? | **The sign of the tunneling current.** This is what makes an automated approach safe to think about |

If the points are not a straight line, or the reading does not move at all, **stop and record it** —
that is a fault in the current path and it matters more than anything else on this list.

# BLOCK G — counts to amps at R23, if there is time (about 20 min)

**Why:** every current figure in the project depends on a constant we have argued about. The
schematic says the answer is 4.096 V, not the 10.24 the Python tools use. One measurement confirms
it.

With the preamp connected and settled, **at the same moment**:

1. Meter on **R23** (either pad), reading DC volts.
2. Run `python Code/pc/stm_console.py ADCR`.

Record both numbers together, and do it at **two well-separated points** if you can make the
preamp output move (for example before and after grounding the shield).

That gives volts per count directly and settles the constant.

# BLOCK H — optional, if the bench work finishes early

## H1. Check the rebuild parts are actually in hand

The preamp rebuild is the next big step if the shield does not fix things. Confirm we physically
have: the **spare preamp board**, a **100 MOhm resistor**, a **PTFE standoff** (Keystone 11301 is
the part Berard names), **2-part epoxy** (not cyanoacrylate), and **fresh IPA**.

Anything missing is a shipping delay, so it is worth knowing today.

## H2. Practice cutting tips

**No tip has ever been prepared in this project.** It is on the open list and it takes practice, so
a quiet half hour is a good time to start.

Berard's method: pull the 0.25 mm tungsten wire with pliers while cutting at an angle with flush
cutters. Tungsten is harder than the cutters so the result looks rough — that is normal and it
still works. Cut several and keep the ones that look sharpest under magnification.

**Do not install one today.** Nothing is ready for a tip, and an installed tip only adds risk to
everything above.
