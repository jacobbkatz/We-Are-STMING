**The limits were 20 mA.** Answered: 20 mA was the preamp-alone setting, for a board drawing about
4 mA per rail; **the controller has never been run at 20 mA**, and its 2026-08-29 bring-up used
200 mA.

**Refined minutes later, `SAID`, Jacob: "one is at 20mA one is at 200mA".** **The limits were NOT
equal: one channel at 20 mA, the other at 200 mA.** That explains the event more directly than the
rail-to-rail argument below: **the 20 mA channel limited and the 200 mA channel did not.** Jacob set
both to 200 mA. Which channel had the 20 mA limit was not stated; both of the controller's measured
draws exceed 20 mA, so either would have limited.

**Still not reported:** **which channel** went into current mode, and how the two channels are
joined.

**Leading explanation, now SUPPORTED by the limit setting but with the wiring still unproven: the
limits were still at 20 mA from the preamp tests.** Every
preamp run on 2026-09-15 used a 20 mA limit. **The controller's own draw was measured at the first
power-up on 2026-08-29: V++ 60 mA, V-- 36 mA at ±18 V** (`docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF_SUMMARY.md` §A.1.11).
Both exceed 20 mA. **Why only one channel limits** is consistent with this: V++ feeds three
regulators and every digital rail, so it hits the limit first and collapses; most of the V-- load is
op-amp and DAC supply current that flows rail to rail, and it falls away once the positive rail is
starved, which can leave the negative channel below its limit and at 18 V.

**The other live explanation is a wiring fault at the supply**: a channel reversed, or the two
channels not stacked in series with their junction on AGND. **That is why the instruction given was
to prove the wiring before raising the limit**, not after:

1. Plug out of U19. Both limits to about **200 mA**, the value the 2026-08-29 bring-up used.
2. Outputs on, plug hanging free. Black probe on the plug's middle wire; the outer wires must read
   about **+18 V and −18 V**.
3. Outputs off. On the board, beep each outer U19 pin to **U18's middle leg**, pad 2, the V-- input
   (`docs/WIRING.md` §7). **That pin must receive the −18 V wire.**
4. Only then plug in and power. **Expect roughly 60 mA and 36 mA**, plus a few mA for the preamp.
   **A channel still in current mode at 200 mA is a real fault: switch off and do not retry.**

**Damage assessment, reasoned rather than measured:** with a limit that low, very little power
reached the board. Running briefly with the negative rail present and the positive rail starved has
**not been checked chip by chip** against every datasheet.

| Upstream firmware's assumption | LOW retracts — Mech Panda's build, not proof for ours |

**Result: UNKNOWN from the repository.** Either unknown flips the answer.

**Why not just guess:** the woodpecker steps the motor with Z at the "retracted" end. **Told the wrong
end, every step happens with the tip fully extended**, and one step can take it from no current to a
crash.

**What was built instead: `--z-retracted unknown` in `Code/pc/stm_approach.py`.** Z parks at midscale
for every motor step; each cycle sweeps midscale → 10000, then midscale → 50000; the first contact
shows which half extends, the tool retracts to the other end and prints which to use next time. It
first checks for contact already at midscale, and if it finds it, **stops without guessing.** Safe
while one motor step is well under half the Z range.

**Tests: 40 → 50, all passing.** The ten new ones cover both true orientations, the midscale
invariant, the no-contact and contact-at-midscale cases, a throw mid-run, and **the safety property:
never more than one Z step past contact.** **Each was shown to fail on purpose**, in a scratch copy:

| Fault injected | Caught by |
|---|---|
| Motor steps with Z at the low end instead of midscale | 5 tests, including the penetration test: **"went 2400 past contact"** |
| Direction learned backwards | both learn-direction tests |
| No contact check at midscale | the contact-at-midscale test |
| None: unmodified copy | all pass |

**Two defects in the TEST HARNESS were found by doing that, and fixed:**

1. **The simulated microscope only measured penetration when the ADC was read.** A crash during a
   motor step, followed by Z moving away before the next read, went unrecorded. **It now tracks after
   every command.**
2. **Every test gap was an exact multiple of the 2500-count motor step**, so a faulty design landed
   exactly ON contact and never past it. **The safety test and a new control now use uneven gaps**,
   and the control proves the original mode, given the wrong direction, does go past contact.

**A bench alternative, not done:** DSUB1 unplugged, a meter on a quadrant wire against ground, press
the disc centre gently and read the sign of the voltage spike. It would give the poling sign directly,
but it means pressing on the piezo and the improvised holder.

**With a tip fitted, "nothing can be crashed" no longer holds.** Every Z and motor command from here
is a tip hazard.

## 9. Process lessons

- **"Not found" inside an already-open program is not evidence that a tool is missing.** Check the
  machine's own PATH, or the install folder, before installing again.
- **A setting that is right for one test becomes a habit for the next.** Four days of preamp runs at
  a 20 mA limit made 20 mA feel like "what we normally run at". The plan's power-on step did give a
  different limit, but **never said the current setting was wrong for the controller**, and
  **wrongly said its draw had never been recorded**, so there was no number on the page to make
  20 mA look small. Both are now in the plan.
- **A tool that has only ever run on one kind of computer carries that computer's assumptions.** The
  commit guard had been "proved able to fail" on 2026-09-09, but only where `python3` exists. On a
  new machine it would have failed for the wrong reason, and the message would have pointed at the
  documents rather than at the interpreter.
