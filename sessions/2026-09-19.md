## 3. The springs, and the reversal

**Jacob supplied the Amazon listing.** FOCMKEAS, **304 stainless, wire Ø0.50 mm, OD Ø3.00 mm, free
length 300 mm**, double hook, ordered 2026-06-21. Mean coil Ø2.50 mm, **spring index C = 5.0**,
~600 close-wound coils.

    k = G*d^4 / (8*D^3*n) = 69e9 * (0.5e-3)^4 / (8 * (2.5e-3)^3 * 600) = 57.5 N/m each

**The rate had been UNKNOWN since the BOM** and `docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md` says the platform
*"has never been weighed"*. **Neither was ever needed.** One part number closed it.

### The arithmetic that proves the springs were shut

A close-wound extension spring does not extend **at all** until the load beats its initial tension:

    F_i = pi*d^3*tau_i/(8*D),  tau_i 103-152 MPa at C = 5  ->  2.0-3.0 N each  ->  610-910 g total

**And the check that needs no faith in that band:** at 173 N/m, the measured **2 mm of droop implies
a hanging assembly of 35 g**. There are **50 g of nickels in the cups alone**, plus a Ø200 x 6 mm
printed disc, the scan head and the preamp. **35 g is impossible, so the springs were not
extending.** Jacob described exactly that when he measured it on 2026-09-18:

> *"the drop is maybe 2mm its barley hanging the springs are not very stretchy"*

**"Barely hanging" was literally correct.**

### It was written down a day earlier and then contradicted

**`docs/INVENTORY.md`'s spring row already carried Jacob's 2026-09-18 bench words** — *"these
springs are more strechy than I thought adding more wieght could help I think"* — **and the
initial-tension hypothesis in writing**, in the same row. **Both were right. The 2026-09-19
analysis contradicted them without re-reading the row.**

**`CLAUDE.md` §3b is about searching before recording an UNKNOWN. This is the neighbouring failure:
searching before OVERRIDING something already recorded.** The lesson is the same one and the grep
was the same length.

## 4. The evidence the springs have now opened

**Jacob had to RAISE the platform after adding the coin.** A close-wound spring only sags once the
load beats its initial tension — below the knee the coils are shut and nothing moves. **So 356.2 g
crossed it**, consistent with the 610-910 g band for a 250-400 g bare platform.

He then trimmed the droop out with the height adjusters and put the damping plate back over the
magnets. **That is the correct order and it costs nothing**, because:

> **`f0` does not depend on the droop.** Once the coils are open,
> **`f0 = (1/2pi)*sqrt(k_total/M)`** — the rate and the mass, nothing else. The droop is a
> consequence. **Trimming it out moves the resting position and leaves the rate untouched.**

| Total mass | `f0` | 5 Hz transmitted |
|---|---|---|
| 0.7 kg | 2.50 Hz | x0.33 |
| 0.8 kg | 2.34 Hz | x0.28 |
| 1.0 kg | 2.09 Hz | x0.21 |

**Against a stage that was contributing nothing this morning, that is a three- to five-fold cut in
the dominant noise. NONE OF IT IS MEASURED.** The bounce test in `STATUS.md` settles it in thirty
seconds and is tonight's first job.

**Do not add more mass.** The adjusters are at maximum so another sag could not be corrected, and
0.8 to 1.2 kg buys only 20% on `f0`.

## 5. Two real bugs in `check_facts.py`

**Found by retiring a value: the checker reported `clean` while FOURTEEN stale copies of `11 Hz`
sat in five live documents.**

**Bug 1, a dead qualifier.** The RETIRED row was phrased `` `11 Hz` as the **suspension vertical
resonance** ``. The qualifier narrows a match to lines carrying those words, and **no document uses
that phrase**, so the row could never fire. **The checker caught this one itself** —
`check_dead_qualifiers()` exists because the same thing happened on 2026-09-09.

**Bug 2, `[\d.]+` matches a bare full stop.**

```python
num = re.search(r'[\d.]+', replacement)     # the corrected value, to skip correction lines
if num and num.group(0) in line:
    continue
```

The replacement text was prose — *"UNKNOWN pending a bench measurement. The springs are..."* — and
**the pattern matched the full stop after "measurement" before reaching a digit.** `"." in line` is
true for nearly every line, **so nearly every hit was silently discarded.** The one it did report,
`docs/showcase.html:658`, was **the only stale line in the repository with no full stop in it.**

**Every earlier RETIRED row had a short numeric replacement — `119 nA`, `10.24 V` — so none of them
ever reached a dot first and the bug sat unexposed.**

**Fixed to `\d[\d.]*` and commented at the site. Verified by re-introducing the fault:**

| Version | Result with 14 stale values present |
|---|---|
| `[\d.]+` | **`check_facts: clean.`** |
| `\d[\d.]*` | **`check_facts: 14 possible stale value(s).`** |

**All fourteen then corrected** across `STATUS.md`, `docs/INVENTORY.md`, two bench HTML pages,
`docs/showcase.html` and `sessions/README.md`.

## 6. The noise tool for tonight

**`sessions/data/2026-09-18-bench/scripts/spectrum.py` hardcodes `C:\Users\jacob\OneDrive\...` and
`COM3`. It fails on the first two lines on any other machine**, and tonight's run is on Nuh's.

**`Code/pc/stm_noise_spectrum.py`** is the same arithmetic with the machine taken out: finds the
Teensy by USB vendor ID, takes `--port`, **writes raw samples to CSV**, and only ever reads.

**Getting the MEASURE right took four iterations, each driven by a failing test:**

| Attempt | Why it was wrong |
|---|---|
| Raw band means | The white floor sits under every band. A run at sd 47 reads "7x better" everywhere with no mechanical change |
| Band divided by the floor | If the broadband noise is fixed and the mechanics untouched, the ratio leaps and the tool reports a rise that is not there |
| Floor subtracted from mean AMPLITUDE in quadrature | Biased at low signal-to-noise, and **biased more when the floor is large** — the wrong direction for tonight |
| **Floor subtracted in POWER** | `E[|signal+noise|^2] = A^2 + E[|noise|^2]` exactly. Unbiased |

**And the control band had 10 sparse lines.** Line power is exponentially distributed, so that is a
**32% standard error on the divisor** — enough to turn "nothing changed" into a 1.3x rise. **Now 61
lines, about 6%.**

**One thing could NOT be fixed from here.** 2026-09-18's raw samples were never kept, so its side of
any cross-night comparison uses a different estimator. On synthetic data with a known truth of 10.0
counts, this reads **7.4 at a high floor and 9.7 at a low one** — biasing toward *"it got worse"* by
up to 30%. **The tool now refuses a confident cross-night verdict when the floors differ by more
than 2x**, and points at the comparison that is reliable: **`--compare` on two runs from the same
night**, still versus someone stamping the floor. Same floor, same gain, same estimator, nothing
reconstructed.

**`Code/pc/stm_noise_spectrum_test.py`** builds four worlds with known truth and checks the verdict
in each, **including the trap case where the floor falls 7x and the mechanics are unchanged.** All
four pass and the trap is shown being caught.
