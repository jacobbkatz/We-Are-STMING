## 3. Measurements

**Conditions.** Bench supply only, never the controller. No tip lead, no box, no shield. LED1–LED4
not relevant — the controller was not powered. **Jacob was within a metre for every reading**, which
matters and is discussed in §3.4. Meter on DC volts, black probe on the supply ground junction, red
probe on the free end of the orange OUT lead.

### 3.1 Continuity on the built node, unpowered

**Reported verbatim:** `1: beep · 2: no beep · 3: no beep · 4: no beep · 5: 220 ohms · 6: all open`.
Later confirmed separately: **the standoff is silent to both the grey and the tan supply leads.**

**Every value is a pass. No reading indicated a fault.**

> **Honest limitation, and it is the second time this has happened.** **Six items were reported
> against the eight checks** on [`docs/preamp_input_node_tests.html`](../docs/preamp_input_node_tests.html),
> and the `220 ohms` sits at item 5 where that page puts the −15 V silence check. **The numbering was
> never reconciled at the bench.** The 220 Ω is almost certainly PAD1 to hole 3, which is R1, because
> there is no plausible 220 Ω path from the standoff to a supply rail — **but that is a reconstruction,
> not a record.** A later session needing the individual rows should **re-measure.**

### 3.2 First power-on, ±5 V rails, 20 mA limit

| | |
|---|---|
| Supply current, both channels | **3 mA**, matching |
| OUT lead | **3.5 mV**, stable |
| **Input current** | **~35 pA** |

**A control was taken before the near-zero reading was believed:** the same meter on the grey lead
read **5.0 V**. `CLAUDE.md`'s "take a control measurement" rule, applied at the bench. **Without it
the 3.5 mV would have been indistinguishable from a probe that had fallen off.**

### 3.3 The timed run, ±15 V rails, 20 mA limit. Minute zero 17:49 UTC

| Minute | OUT lead | Input current | Supply current |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 5 mV, wandering | ~50 pA | 4 mA |
| ~2 | 0.9 mV | ~9 pA | — |
| 5 | **−0.3 mV** | ~3 pA | 4 mA |
| 10 | **−0.6 mV** | **~6 pA** | 4 mA |

**Stopped at 10 minutes by choice** — Jacob broke for lunch and powered down. **The 20, 45 and 60
minute points were not taken.**

**The sign flip is not information.** A few tenths of a mV sits inside both the meter's last digit
and the op-amp's own input offset voltage. **Do not chase a sign the magnitude cannot carry.**

**The reading FELL and crossed zero.** All of the movement was in the first two minutes; between
minute 5 and minute 10 it moved 0.3 mV, which is one count of the meter.

### 3.4 An accidental control: a gloved touch on the standoff

| | |
|---|---|
| Nitrile glove brushing the standoff | **0.05 V** = **500 pA** |
| Left alone afterwards | **0.9 mV** = ~9 pA, recovered on its own |
| Repeated | **0.05 V again** — reproducible |

**It recovers completely**, which is what a working high-impedance node does: no new leakage path and
no sticky offset.

### 3.5 Not measured

The 20, 45 and 60 minute points. Anything with a tip lead, a box or a shield. The supply current at
the moment the rails wandered — **that was the discriminator and it was missed.** The standoff's
actual diameter: **there are no calipers.** Which op-amp is fitted.

## 4. New faults

**None on the preamp board. Nothing broke and nothing turned out to be broken.**

**One on the bench supply, unexplained.** Mid-run the output rails "started floating"; Jacob cut the
power, reset the supply, and the rails came back to 15 V and −15 V. **It was the supply, not the
board or the meter** — established only after the fact, by the reset fixing it.

**Cause unknown.** A loose lead at the supply terminals and a glitch in the supply itself are both
live. **The 20 mA limit is not a likely cause**: the board draws 4 mA and never approaches it.
**If it recurs, capture the supply's display and its constant-current indicator at that moment.**
No damage expected: the limit held throughout and a wandering rail in constant-voltage mode sags
rather than overshoots.

## 9. Process lessons

- **The control measurement is what made the headline number believable.** A reading of 3.5 mV on a
  100 MΩ input is indistinguishable from a probe that has fallen off. **Moving the red probe to the
  grey lead and seeing 5.0 V cost twenty seconds** and converted a hopeful zero into a measurement.
  `CLAUDE.md` has carried that rule since 2026-08-31; tonight is the first time it was the difference.
- **Rendering the bench page caught a defect that reading it never would.** The underside toggle had
  been dead in `preamp_ground_repair_map.html` the whole time it was in use. **It was found by opening
  the page in a browser**, which is the documentation equivalent of running the test.
- **Three wrong answers on one part number, and the third had a repository file agreeing with it.**
  The 2026-09-14 correction reasoned that Berard's own board specifies 2.1082 mm for this hole and our
  board matches, so an 11301 must fit. **That is a sound argument from two independent documents, and
  it was wrong.** **Two documents agreeing is not a measurement.** The part was in the room the whole
  time and one offer-up settled it.
- **Six numbers reported against eight checks, for the second night running.** The bench page was
  built specifically to stop this, with a box that assembles the readings into one line. **It was not
  used.** The fix is not another feature; it is asking for the rows back one at a time when they
  matter.

## 10. After the break — boxing, and three decisions

**Written after §1–9. The session continued after lunch and the state changed substantially.**

### 10.1 Two executive decisions by Jacob, both recorded as his

**C2 will NOT be replaced.** *"We will not be replacing C2, executive decision."* The part is not in
the room and not on order. **Safety rule 14 clause (a) is withdrawn**, and the rule now has one
clause: the 45-minute reading. **That is now the only gate between this board and the controller.**

The reasoning is kept on both sides in the rule rather than deleted. **Against:** the fitted tantalum
was refitted with two irons well past the 3 s limit, tantalums fail short, and the controller's
supply can drive a short hard where the 20 mA bench supply cannot. **In favour, and this evidence did
not exist when the rule was written:** C2 reads 4.65–4.7 µF so it has not gone open; it has now run
about 15 minutes at 5 V and 15 V rails with both channels matching at 3–4 mA and nothing warm, where
a badly leaking C2 would have shown as extra current on the negative channel alone; and at 15 V on a
35 V part it sits at 43% of rating, inside the conventional 50% derating.

**`check_facts.py` caught the consequence immediately** — `docs/NEXT_SESSION_PLAN.md` still cited
safety rule 14 for replacing C2, which no longer matched the rule. **Grepped for the class rather
than fixing the line, and found five more live sites.** All updated.

### 10.2 The shielding is finished and metered — V1 is CLOSED

**`SAID`: the preamp box is wrapped in copper and all shielding has been metered end to end.**
V1 had been open since 2026-09-06 and **every shield conclusion in this project rested on it.**

**Two places are aluminium by decision**, and both are now written down so nobody reads "copper
everywhere" next month and concludes the build is wrong:

| Where | Wrap | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Motor mount extenders | Aluminium, then copper over it | **The one place the rule still bites.** Copper on aluminium is a galvanic couple in humidity. **Far from the input node, acts over months.** Watch, do not rebuild |
| Piezo holding block | Aluminium only | **Defensible and matches Mech Panda.** It is **not** the scan head shield cover — that is `6_shield_cover.stl`, a separate part, and the copper-only instruction is about that |

**`docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md` §3 was amended.** It read *"use copper everywhere, do not use
aluminium at all"*, and the instrument does not follow that in two places. **An absolute rule the
hardware breaks teaches the reader that the rules here are decorative** — the same lesson §3bb
learned on 2026-09-09. The technical table is unchanged; the exceptions are listed under it.

| **Verdict** | **Closing the box does not touch the input node** |

**The v2 boss and the standoff are the same point, confirmed independently.** Board→box offset is

**The standing instruction never to print v2 now has arithmetic behind it, not just a note.**

**One hazard found and raised:** the base has a **Ø3.600 mm support post at board (16.197, 7.615)**,
and the four repair wires run across that half of the underside. On the drawing the nearest clears by
about **0.6 mm**. Jacob inspected and reported the wires visually good; **whether that inspection
specifically covered the post was not stated.**

### 10.4 The board is in the box, and the tip lead is cleared but deferred

**Mounted on two M2 screws into the printed pilots.** Both post-mount checks passed: standoff to

**Safety rule 0b's tip-lead clause is CLEARED** — it gated the tip lead on the bare board being
