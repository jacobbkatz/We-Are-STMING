### 3.7 The pull-back that found the real fault

**17:29:38 to 17:33:42 UTC, −9,000 steps in 400-step commands**, to recover travel before resetting
the gap by hand. **Jacob, at 17:33: "the screw just came away."** The plate did not follow the screw

**Verified under motor drive at 18:03:52 to 18:04:12: +400 steps then −400 steps, and Jacob

### 3.9 Two false contacts from the approach tool, and the noise that caused the second

**Run at 18:05:29 to 18:10:38, 1000 steps, no contact — but it was deaf.** The tool sets its
threshold from the noise it measures in its first seconds: **it measured 1,498 counts RMS (4.68 nA)
and therefore chose a threshold of 8,990 counts (28 nA).** **A tunnelling current would not have
stopped it.**

**A bias sweep then showed there was no current at all**, so nothing had been touched:

| Sample bias | Mean, 12 `ADCR` | sd |
|---|---|---|
| 0 V | −88.3 | 274.3 |
| −0.5 V | −101.3 | 213.4 |
| +0.5 V | +27.8 | 306.7 |

**Shifts of −13 and +116 counts, both inside their own standard errors.** At 320.5 counts/nA a
1 nA junction would move the mean by about 1,600 counts per volt, with opposite signs. **This bias
sweep is the cheapest test this project has for "is that reading real", and it should be run at
every candidate contact.**

**Re-run at 18:12:26 with `--threshold 1500` fixed: it fired at ZERO motor steps**, reading +2,649
against a baseline of −266, with the measured noise at 1,228 counts. **A 2.4-sigma excursion, passed
by the 2-of-3 confirmation.** Z was left at 50000 by the tool and **re-parked at 32768 immediately**,
because the direction it printed is not trusted.

**Both false contacts are the same class of defect: the tool tests magnitude only.**

> **Recommendation, not implemented.** At a candidate contact the tool should **confirm with the
> bias**: set the sample to the opposite polarity and require the reading to flip, then to 0 V and
> require it to return to baseline. **A real junction follows the bias; noise does not.** That would
> have rejected both of today's false contacts, where a sign test alone would have caught only the
> first.

   counts was 2.6 sigma, and it tripped within 20 motor steps. **`ADCR` averages; `GSTS` field 5 does
   not. Never compare a threshold against the wrong one.**

|---|---|

**The reading follows the bias polarity, which noise cannot do, and the signs match the dummy

driving the sample away from the tip**, which is why 8,000 steps found nothing.

current to about (13 V)/(100 MΩ) = 130 nA regardless of the contact.

### 3.14 The Z scale, measured against the motor

**The only mechanical ruler available is the motor: one step is 317.5 um / 2048 = 155 nm of screw.**
Retracting in blocks of 20 steps and re-finding the junction each time, 19:48:47:

| Motor steps retracted | Onset Z |
|---|---|
| 0 | 28,200 to 40,600 |
| 20 | 37,800 to 44,000 |
| 40 | 44,000 to 44,600 |
| **60 and beyond** | **out of Z range** |

**So roughly 250 Z counts per motor step, and the whole 10,000-50,000 sweep is covered by about 50
motor steps.**

**What that means depends entirely on the lever ratio, which is still unmeasured:**

| If the tip is this far from the pivot line | Lever ratio | One motor step at the tip | Z scale | Full Z range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 mm (the design) | 40 | 3.9 nm | 0.016 nm/count | ~1.0 um |
| 0.3 mm | 133 | 1.2 nm | 0.005 nm/count | ~310 nm |
| 0.1 mm | 400 | 0.39 nm | 0.0016 nm/count | ~100 nm |

**At the design ratio the current decays a decade per 3 nm, which is 30x too slow for vacuum
tunnelling. At a ratio near 300-400 it lands close to the textbook decade per 0.1 nm.** Jacob's own
reading is that the tip sits in the middle of the line through the two ball ends, which favours the
larger ratios. **Measuring where the tip is relative to that line is now the single most valuable
measurement left**, and it needs a straightedge, not electronics.

> **An earlier estimate in this session, that the Z scale was about 0.0002 nm/count and the range
> 13 nm, is RETRACTED.** It came from assuming the textbook decay applies and solving for the scale.
> The motor calibration contradicts it by two orders of magnitude, and `docs/INVENTORY.md` was
> corrected within the session.

**A second mechanical finding: backlash.** After reversing the motor, **100 to 250 steps were needed
before the plate moved again** — twice, measured by how far the junction failed to reappear.
**Any procedure that reverses the motor must expect a few hundred steps of lost motion.**

### 3.15 The I-V curve: a barrier, not a short

**Eight rounds of "find the junction, then immediately sweep the bias", 19:54:51.** Interleaving the
polarities means drift cannot fake a slope. **Round 7, the cleanest:**

| Sample bias | Current, nA | Junction resistance |
|---|---|---|
| ±0.05 V | 0.85 | 59 MΩ |
| ±0.10 V | 2.0 | 50 MΩ |
| ±0.15 V | 3.3 | 45 MΩ |
| ±0.25 V | 7.5 | 33 MΩ |
| ±0.35 V | 14.6 | 24 MΩ |
| ±0.50 V | 31.7 | 16 MΩ |

**Symmetric in both polarities to about 10%, and superlinear: the current goes as roughly V^2.** A
metallic short would be ohmic and flat; an open circuit would give nothing. **A barrier junction is
what tunnelling is.** Round 3 of the same run shows the contrast — pinned at every voltage with
10,800 counts even at zero bias, which is what a hard contact looks like.

### 3.19 One motor step is about one decade of current

**Four fine sweeps at 25-count resolution in the settled state, 20:05:57:**

| Sweep | Rise measured | Counts per decade |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 600 -> 24,400 counts over 200 Z counts | **125** |
| 3 | 1,480 -> 27,800 over 300 | **236** |
| 1 | 2,300 -> 25,200 over 450 | **433** |
| 2 | 1,250 -> saturation over 475 | **340** |

**Median about 250 Z counts per decade — the same as the 250 Z counts per motor step measured in
§3.14. So one motor step changes the current by roughly tenfold.**

> **What this says about tunnelling, stated carefully.** A vacuum tunnel gap changes tenfold per
> 0.1 nm. One motor step is 155 nm of screw, so for this junction to be vacuum tunnelling the lever
> ratio would have to be about **1,550** — the tip within **26 um** of the pivot line. **That is
> possible but implausibly exact.** The alternative fits everything measured: **the tip rests on a
> thin insulating film — contamination, or oxide — and Z presses it rather than changing a vacuum
> gap.** That gives a barrier-shaped I-V (§3.15), a slow current-versus-Z, and easy saturation into
> hard contact. **Neither is proven. The straightedge measurement decides it.**

### 3.22 THE IMAGING ATTEMPT — the loop works, the image does not

**Jacob, back at the bench: *"try more latteral scans I want to see if an image is possible today
... try your hardest"*.** What follows is the whole attempt, including the two analyses that killed
results I had already half-believed.

**Constant height does not work here.** Holding Z and rastering saturates within one to six lines:
**the surface's own roughness is far larger than the range of gap the current can report at a fixed
height.** Crumpled gold leaf is not flat at this scale.

**So the loop had to move Z, and `CCON` is barred by safety rule 8.** A PC-side constant-current
loop was written instead — `Code/pc/stm_feedback_scan.py` — with Z clamped to 12000-48000, at most
300 counts of Z per pixel, counters for saturated and clamped pixels, an abort, and a retract on
every exit path including Ctrl-C. **It was tested against a simulated junction first**
(`Code/pc/stm_feedback_scan_test.py`): it holds a flat surface, follows a 600-count bump to within
20, clamps instead of running away when the surface is out of reach, retreats from hard contact,
and **runs into a clamp when the sign of the gain is reversed on purpose.** That last check first
passed while the fault was present, because it only looked at the high clamp; a reversed loop runs
to the LOW one.

**A speed fault found on the way.** The first images took 38 to 60 s each. Timing showed the median
pixel is **1.7 ms** but about **1.5% of reads get no reply and cost up to 3 s each**, which is where
all the time went. `fast_read()` now gives up after 0.12 s, writes four newlines to resynchronise
the firmware's four-character reader, and retries. **Images then took 4.5 to 6.2 s with zero lost
reads.**

**Results, four images of the same patch at +-400 X counts, 21 x 12 pixels:**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Trace against retrace, per image | r = **+0.09, +0.01, 0.00, 0.00** |
| Image against image, forward | between **−0.09 and +0.27** |
| Corrugation left after removing the tilt | **13 to 20 counts** |

**Nothing reproduces. No image.**

> **A result I nearly believed, and the check that killed it.** The earlier slow pair of images
> correlated at **r = +0.75**, which looks like a real topograph. **Dropping the first line of each
> image took it to +0.22, and dropping two took it to +0.04.** The entire agreement was the
> feedback loop's settling transient on the first line — +712, +521, +330 counts in both images,
> because the procedure is identical, not because the sample is. **Across the other lines the mean
> correlation is +0.09 +- 0.44.**

**One thing that IS reproducible, and it is not the sample: the X-to-Z coupling.** Holding the
current while X moves needs **−0.17 counts of Z per count of X** (−0.163, −0.179, −0.198, −0.182
across the four images, forward) and about −0.09 backward, the difference being loop lag. **That is
a fixed tilt between tip and sample, not topography** — it is the ramp that detrending removes, and
over a +-6000 scan it alone demands more than 1,000 counts of Z, which is why the wide scans
saturated even at 300 counts per pixel.

**What would have to change for an image:** a rigid tip holder, a sharper tip, and either a slower
drift or the firmware's own loop. **The PC loop tracks well** — it held the current to about 0.06 of
a decade — **so the electronics and the software are not what is missing.**

### 3.23 DOES THE OPERATOR SITTING THERE MATTER? No. A hand near the head does

**Jacob's question, and a cued A/B/A/B: Claude posted SIT or STEP BACK, Jacob moved at once, and
each block ran exactly 30 s at about 3,600 readings per second.** Z retracted, bias 0 V, no junction.
Alternating blocks mean a slow drift cannot masquerade as an effect.

| Block, 30 s each | Noise, single conversions | In current |
|---|---|---|
| Seated, block 1 | 186.2 counts | 0.581 nA |
| A metre away, block 1 | 188.0 | 0.587 |
| Seated, block 2 | 186.9 | 0.583 |
| A metre away, block 2 | 188.2 | 0.587 |
| **Hand about 5 cm from the scan head** | **221.5** | **0.691** |

**Seated against a metre away: +1.6 +- 0.3 counts — under 1%, and in the WRONG direction**, very
slightly quieter with Jacob in the chair. Resolvable only because each pool holds 58 seconds.
**Practical answer: sitting at the bench is fine.** A body nearby may even shield a little.

**A hand 5 cm from the head: +35.0 +- 2.1 counts, 16 sigma**, which is **119 counts, 0.37 nA, added
in quadrature — about a third of a tunnelling current.** **Hands away from the head for any capture
that matters.**

**This refines safety rule 9**, which says no preamp measurement is valid while anyone is within a
metre, on a figure of 20 to 50 nA measured when IC1's +IN floated. **Tonight, on the repaired board:
a whole seated body at normal distance is worth about 1% of the noise, and a hand at 5 cm about
0.37 nA. The rule's spirit holds for hands; its metre is not supported by this measurement.**
**Not rewritten here — that is Jacob and Nuh's call.**

**One caveat, stated because it could matter: this was measured with the tip retracted and no
junction.** A live tunnel gap is a far higher impedance and could couple more.

## 6. False trails

- **"The magnets behind the sample plate are pushing the gold off."** Gold is diamagnetic; the force
  is at most a few percent of the leaf's own weight against a strong magnet. **Ruled out by
  arithmetic**, and the leaf went down afterwards.
- **"The noise is charge left on the input node and will decay in minutes."** It did decay, but over
  about **half an hour**, not the six minutes tested. The six-minute test looked like a refutation
  and was not.
- **"Raise the threshold above the noise and keep approaching."** Twice wrong: the first threshold
  was below the single-conversion noise, and the framing of the auto threshold as deaf was wrong.
- **"Positive `MTMV` approaches, because the plate is a lever."** Held by the bench and by Claude for
  most of the evening. **Cost roughly two hours and 8,000 motor steps of driving the wrong way.**
- **"The lateral scan shows surface structure."** Four lines, one correlated pair out of six.

## 9. Process lessons

- **The bias flip is the cheapest truth test this instrument has.** It rejected two false contacts
  and confirmed the real one, in seconds, without moving anything. **Any candidate signal should be
  bias-flipped before it is believed.**
- **"Which way does the lever go" was answered by a crash, not by argument.** Both sides reasoned
  from geometry all evening; the answer arrived the moment something touched. **A cheap physical
  measurement — a straightedge — was available the whole time and was never taken.**
- **A mechanical assumption nobody had checked cost two sessions.** Nothing in the repository said
  the plate rests on the motor screw, because nobody imagined it might not.
- **Claude compared a threshold against the wrong noise figure and told Jacob it was 13 sigma when it
  was 2.6.** Averaged and single readings differ by the square root of the averaging. **Say which
  one a number came from.**
- **Two of Claude's numbers were retracted within the same session**, both stated too confidently on
  first pass: the Z scale, and the "deaf threshold". **Stating the uncertainty up front would have
  cost nothing.**
