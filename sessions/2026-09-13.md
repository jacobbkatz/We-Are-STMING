### 3.2 C2 marking and rating

Printing, read under the magnifier: an up-arrow logo, `475V`, `80E02`.

`475` = 4.7 µF. `V` = **35 V** in the KYOCERA AVX TAJ rated-voltage code (2.5 V e, 4 V G, 6.3 V J,
10 V A, 16 V C, 20 V D, 25 V E, 35 V V, 50 V T) — read from the TAJ datasheet with PyMuPDF. The
letter code matches AVX; **the up-arrow logo was not identified**, so the manufacturer is not
confirmed. 15 V is 43% of 35 V.

### 3.3 C2 rework

- Removed with **two irons** at 350 °C, a blob of fresh solder on each end, lifted straight up.
- Pads wicked flat.
- **The same part refitted, rotated 180°** — stripe now on the near pad (toward JP1). No spare
  tantalum was accessible and the team did not want to wait for a replacement.
- Exposure time during removal was not recorded. **Vishay TN-0004: "If it is necessary to keep the
  iron on longer than 3 s, replace the component with a fresh device."** Two-iron removal of a 6032
  part almost certainly exceeded that. **Treat the reused part as temporary.**

After refitting:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Capacitance across C2's pads, on the board | **4.65 µF** |
| C2 far end ↔ JP1 hole 5 | beep |
| C2 near end ↔ JP1 hole 1 | **silent — on the spare AND on the old board** |

4.65 µF is C2 alone: C4 does not sit in parallel on these boards, because C4's ground end is also
unconnected (§3.6). Within ±10% of 4.7 µF. A capacitance reading also proves both C2 joints conduct,
so **the silent beep at the near end was not a bad joint**.

## 4. New faults

### 4.1 The fabricated preamp boards have no ground pour — IC1's reference input floats

**Both boards, measured.** An upstream conversion defect: the design has the pour, the manufacturing
files do not. **Nobody here caused it**, and who converted the Eagle design to KiCad is not recorded.

Consequences:

1. **IC1 +IN floats**, so the TIA has no 0 V reference. The output follows wherever that node
   drifts. **No preamp reading in this project measured input current.**
2. **C2, C3 and C4 decouple nothing.** Their ground ends float. Only C1 (+15 V) was working.
3. **The old board's reversed C2 was probably never reverse-biased.** Its ground end floats (bench
   beep, both boards), so nothing held its cathode at 0 V. This de-escalates the fire-risk wording
   in `STATUS.md` fault 1d for the old board. It is derived from the copper, not measured with power.
4. **JP1 pin 4** is not a separate routing defect; it is the same missing pour.

### 4.2 C3's ground-via stub is 0.076 mm from the +15 V pad

A manufacturing-clearance hazard, found in the gerbers. **Untested.** Before scraping or wiring
that via: C3's ground pad ↔ JP1 hole 2 must be silent.

## 9. Process lessons

**Labels in design files are intent, not copper.** Every source in this repository listed these
pads as `GND` — the Eagle netlist, the gerber X2 attributes, `docs/WIRING.md`. None of them can say
whether copper joins them. This is the same class of error as `CLAUDE.md` §3d, where the BOM was
read as an inventory: **a record of what was meant, read as a record of what exists.**

**The clues were already written down.** Pin 4 unrouted (2026-08-31), "zero copper pours on either
layer" (2026-09-06), "no guard ring" (2026-09-09). Nobody asked what the missing copper was meant to
connect. Claude also printed the underside copper file early this session and did not trace it.

**It surfaced because Nuh reported an unexpected silence on both boards** instead of assuming a bad
joint, and then **challenged the finding hard**. The answer to that challenge was a check designed not
to depend on the hard-to-hit target: scrape the via, then via-to-hole-1 silent.

**At the bench, one exact path beats a menu of options.** The multi-option instructions early in the
session caused real confusion. What worked: one probe point at a time, a reply format, and a
to-scale drawing generated from the manufacturing files rather than drawn by hand.
