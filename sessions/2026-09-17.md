## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The Z sweep steps straight over the tunnelling regime — NEW, and a tip hazard

**`Code/pc/stm_approach.py` line 68: `DEFAULT_Z_STEP = 200`.** The 2026-09-16 approach was run
without `--z-step`, so it used that default.

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Z displacement per DAC count | **0.0104 nm** | `docs/FACTS.md`, **INFER** — Berard's 34 nm/V disc, **not our scanner** |
| Sweep step, as run | 200 counts = **2.08 nm per sample point** | CALC |
| Noise floor, quiet capture | 12.7 counts = **0.040 nA** | MEAS 2026-09-16 §3.2h |
| Contact threshold used | **4.7 nA** | 2026-09-16 §3.11 |
| Decades between them | 4.7 / 0.040 = **2.07 decades** | CALC |
| Tunnelling current vs distance | **~10x per 0.1 nm** | REF, standard figure, not ours |
| **So the detectable window is** | **2.07 × 0.1 = 0.21 nm wide** | CALC |
| **Samples the sweep takes inside it** | **0.10** | CALC |

**Read plainly: the tool samples every 2.08 nm, and the entire range in which a tunnelling current is
both above the noise and below the threshold is 0.21 nm wide.** A sample point lands inside that
window roughly **one approach in ten**. The other nine times the tip goes from reading nothing to
being **in contact with the surface between two consecutive samples**, and the tool's first sight of
any current is a tip that has already touched.

**The fix is a command-line flag, not a code change.** `--z-step` already exists
(`stm_approach.py` line 433). **`--z-step 5`** gives 0.052 nm per point, about 4 samples inside the
window. **Cost: 6,554 points per half sweep at the measured 0.5 ms per read ≈ 3.3 s.** That is
affordable precisely because the read time was fixed on 2026-09-16.

**What this does NOT explain.** It does not explain the failed approach: nothing was ever detected,
and this fault would have produced a *crash*, not silence. **It is a hazard waiting for the approach
that does reach the surface.**

**The load-bearing assumption is INFERRED.** 0.0104 nm/count comes from Berard's disc. It cuts both
ways and both directions are worth knowing:

- **If our piezo is LESS sensitive than assumed**, each count is fewer nm, the sweep is finer, and
  this problem shrinks — but the **Z half-sweep of 341 nm** shrinks too, and it must stay larger than
  one motor step (38.8–77.5 nm at the three candidate lever ratios). **Coverage fails only if the
  scanner is more than about 4x less sensitive than assumed.** Checked at all three ratios: OK at
  each.
- **If our piezo is MORE sensitive**, the problem is worse than stated.

### 10.1 Is the leaf actually gold? Never tested, and it matters

**New open question, and a gap in `docs/INVENTORY.md` that should have been filled weeks ago: the
gold leaf was not recorded in that file at all.**

**Why it matters, stated precisely.** Gold is the standard STM sample *because it does not oxidise*.
**Imitation leaf — Dutch metal, composition leaf, schlagmetal — is brass**, copper and zinc, and
brass grows a tarnish layer within days. **That layer is an electrical insulator.** It would not stop
first contact, but every image would be of an oxide film that keeps changing rather than of a metal.

**The test chosen is the flame test, and it needs no chemicals at all.** A 2–3 mm scrap in steel
tweezers, a lighter flame for one or two seconds:

| Observation | Meaning | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Stays bright yellow**, may ball up | **Real gold** | Gold does not oxidise at any temperature |
| **Blacks within a second**, maybe a green flash and white smoke | **Brass** | Copper oxidises to black CuO at a few hundred °C, well below melting; the white smoke is zinc oxide |

**Three chemical alternatives were worked out and ranked by hazard**, since Jacob has reagents:
**bleach spot** (safe; brass darkens in minutes, gold never), **vinegar and salt** (gentlest, slowest),
and **nitric acid** — the jeweller's standard and completely decisive, **but its brown fumes are
nitrogen dioxide, acutely toxic with delayed pulmonary effects**, so it is written up as "only if you
already handle nitric, outdoors, with goggles and gloves rated for it", with the note that the flame
test makes it unnecessary.

**Two prohibitions are stated as hard rules on the card:**

1. **Never make aqua regia.** It is the one mixture that *does* dissolve gold, so **it cannot
   distinguish anything**, and it releases chlorine and nitrosyl chloride.
2. **Never mix bleach with acid or with ammonia.**

**And a third that is specific to this project:** **every chemical test happens in a different room
from the instrument**, with hands and tools washed before returning. **Acid mist, chloride and
dissolved metal salts near the amplifier input are exactly the contamination that cost this project
two weeks**, and nothing about testing a scrap of leaf is worth risking it.

### 10.2 The sheets are far bigger than the sample holder

**`SAID` 2026-09-17, Jacob: "I have 4 inch by inch squares left pretty much."** Recorded in
`docs/INVENTORY.md`.

**The arithmetic reframes the problem completely.**

| | |
|---|---|
| One square | 25.4 mm |
| A generous working piece | 5 mm |
| **Pieces per square** | **5 × 5 = 25** |
| **Pieces from four squares** | **about 100** |
| Scanner's full X/Y range | **0.5 µm** (INFER, `docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md`) |
| **A 5 mm piece against that** | **10,000× wider than the tip ever sees** |

**So the budget is roughly 100 attempts, not four**, and the size of the sheet is working *against*
the job rather than for it. **Cutting it down is the fix, not a compromise.**

**Two methods written up, both resting on one principle: never cut unsupported leaf.**

1. **Sandwich and cut.** One square between two sheets of paper — or its own interleaving tissue if
   it has any — cut through the sandwich into 5 mm strips then squares, and **leave each piece in the
   sandwich until the moment it is used.** The other three squares stay sealed.
2. **Gold the tape, then cut the tape.** Lay a 15 mm piece of copper tape back onto its own release
   liner, apply and burnish the gold onto it with no care for the edges, **then cut the
   gold-on-copper composite down to 3–5 mm** and peel it off the liner onto the plate. **Supported
   gold cuts like paper**, and bare leaf is never handled at final size.

**Also added:** static control — metal tweezers and paper rather than plastic tools, no fleece, cut
on glass or ceramic rather than shedding paper.

## 12. Third pass — the card was written in jargon, and that is a real defect

**Jacob, 2026-09-17: *"idk what burnish means or release liner"*.**

**He was right to stop and say so, and it is worth recording as a process failure rather than a
typo.** `CLAUDE.md` opens by saying neither Jacob nor Nuh writes code and that everything must be
explained in plain language. **The card used "burnish" ten times.** It is a gilder's word, it appears
in every gilding guide, and it went straight into a document written for two people who have never
gilded anything. **A procedure the reader cannot parse is not a procedure.**

### What changed

| Was | Now |
|---|---|
| **"burnish"**, 10 occurrences | **"rub it down"**, with the meaning given: *press and smooth with something soft, in small circles, until it lies flat and sticks* |
| **"its own backing liner"** | **"the shiny backing paper the tape came off"**, with a note not to throw it away |
| **Two separate methods** — one for loose leaf, one for backed transfer leaf | **One method, "the flip"**, which works for both. Seven numbered steps |
| No picture | **A four-panel drawing** of the flip: the paper sandwich, peeling the top sheet, turning it over onto the copper, rubbing and lifting the paper away |
| "read before you open the book" | **"before you unwrap any of it"** — there is no book; Jacob has four loose squares |

**A glossary box now sits before the first use of either word, not after.**

### The one piece of physics the drawing has to carry

**Why the gold stays on the copper and not on the paper.** Gold is soft enough that rubbing presses
it into contact with clean metal and the two metals grip each other. **Paper has nothing to grip
with** — it was only ever holding the leaf by static. **That asymmetry is the whole method**, and a
reader who understands it can recover from any step going wrong.

### One correctness point that was missing entirely

**Nothing sticky and no fingers may touch the face of the gold that ends up pointing upward.** That
is the surface the tip has to read, and **adhesive residue or skin oil on it is an insulating film**
— the microscope would be trying to feel through it. **Paper and cotton are fine; tape adhesive and
fingers are not.** Added as its own box.

**Traced through the flip method to confirm it holds:** both faces of the leaf are only ever against
paper, so the method is clean as written.
