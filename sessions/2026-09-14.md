## 9. Process lessons

**`CLAUDE.md` §3c says to compute what an instruction will do before giving it. That was applied to
the hazard and not to the reading.** The dot 4 clearance was computed, checked and drawn to scale
before anyone touched it — and it was right. The expected meter behaviour three rows below it was
copied forward from the previous session and never checked. It took one line of arithmetic to show
it was wrong, and the cost of not doing it was that **a fully working board briefly looked faulty.**

The rule generalises: **compute the expected reading, not just the safety limit.** Someone at a
bench acts on both.

**A form that only offers the outcomes you predicted will lose the outcome you did not.** The bench
page had "Holds" and "Chirp" buttons at holes 2 and 5 and no "Silent". The correct reading was
unrecordable, and it survived only because Jacob typed it in chat. Six readings saved themselves;
the two that mattered most for the hazard did not. **Where a check has a third outcome, the tool
needs a third button — and "none of these" is always a third outcome.**

**Jacob's "there is more space than it looks" was correct, and measuring it was the right
response.** He could see the board; the drawing had two distances collapsed into one label. The
answer was not to override him or to defer to him, but to go back to the gerber and produce both
numbers. That is the third time in this project that someone at the bench has reported something
that contradicted a document and been right.

SINGLE L=6.35MM TIN", **2 off**, $2.09 each. Per `CLAUDE.md` §3d an order confirmation outranks

### 10.2 It is PTFE-based — BENCH, and it closes a distributor disagreement

**Jacob, part in hand: "the standoff has a PTFE base with no metal anywhere except the pole."**

A **top-mount insulated** terminal: the PTFE body sits on the board surface, a pin passes through,
the metal pole stands above. That resolves the contradiction between DigiKey's order line ("TIN", no
insulator mentioned — that is the plating) and RS's "Insulated, PTFE, White" (that is the part).
**The input node gets a real PTFE insulator, which is what Berard was doing.**

### 10.3 I said press fit. It is clearance. Corrected

**What I wrote earlier this evening:** the 11301 needs a Ø2.184 mm hole against our Ø2.108 mm, so it
is a press fit with 0.076 mm of interference, and if it will not seat, open the hole with a #44
drill.

**That was wrong in the direction that causes damage** — it would have had someone drill out a hole
that was already correct, 2.540 mm from the board edge, at the input node.

**What settled it was a file this repository has held since 2026-08-13.** Berard's own Eagle board,
`preamplifier/eagle/tunnelAmp.brd` inside `We-Are-STMING_PCB.zip`:

```
<hole x="2.54" y="7.62" drill="2.1082"/>
```

| | |
|---|---|
| Berard's standoff hole, as drawn | **2.1082 mm** |
| Our fabricated board | **2.108 mm** — identical to within 0.2 µm |
| The part's through-board pin (0.080") | **2.032 mm** |
| **Fit** | **0.076 mm of clearance. It drops in** |

Two things follow. **The Eagle→KiCad conversion did not change this hole** — unlike the ground pour,
it came through intact, so the hole is exactly as designed and Berard fitted an 11301 into it.
And **0.086" (2.184 mm) is Keystone's *recommended* clearance hole, not a requirement**; opening
ours to it would only leave the standoff loose.

### 10.4 Why the wrong answer happened

**The web was searched before the repository was.** `CLAUDE.md` §3b exists for exactly this: *"before
starting any derivation from scratch"*, grep the repo and check `docs/INDEX.md`. I went to Keystone,
DigiKey, RS and Carlton Bates — all blocked or partial — pieced an answer together from a
distributor parametric table, and got the sign of the fit backwards. **The board file that defines
the hole was one `unzip` away**, and `sessions/2026-09-13.md` §3.6 had read that very file the
session before, to find the missing ground pour.

`CLAUDE.md` also names this exact part as the case where a search snippet was believed too early
(the "2.03 mm" that was a length, not a diameter). **That warning was in front of me and I repeated
the pattern on the same part number.**

The distributor table was not useless — it gave the 0.080" pin size that makes the clearance
arithmetic work. **It should have been the cross-check, not the source.**
