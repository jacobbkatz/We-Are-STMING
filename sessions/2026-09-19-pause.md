## 2. What was found, in order of how much it matters

### 2.1 "The controls reproduce better than the scans" is WITHDRAWN

`STATUS.md` and `sessions/2026-09-19-bench.md` §3.14 carried *"the controls reproduce better (image
to image +0.37 against +0.04)"*. Since an X-held control cannot contain surface structure, that was
the load-bearing argument in the imaging case.

**`cas9_xheld2.csv` is a single scan line, not an image.** The run aborted after line one where the
other seven files carry eleven. `scripts/analyze_scans.py` lines 50-51 truncate both images to the
shorter one with no shape check, so **two of the three control correlations were computed over 21
points instead of 231**, comparing one line against another image's first line.

**Like for like, full images only: scans +0.04, controls +0.07. Indistinguishable.**

**The conclusion survives and never needed this.** The real scans do not reproduce — +0.09, +0.18,
−0.15, scattering both sides of zero over full images. The replacement sentence is simpler for
needing no correction: *neither the scans nor the controls reproduce, and the scans show no more
reproducible structure than a control in which the tip never moved across the surface.*

### 2.2 The same defect reaches the wide images, and it is worse there

§3.16's *"image to image +0.56 to +0.86, matched by the X-held controls' +0.56"*. `img_scan_0.csv`
is **1 line** and `img_scan_1.csv` is **2**, against 11. Both scan figures are 21- and 42-point
fragment correlations. **With one complete real wide image there is no valid reproducibility figure
for the wide scans at all.** The controls' +0.56 over a full 231 points is sound, and is a real
result worth keeping: **with X held, the instrument repeats its own pattern strongly.**

**Audited for the class, not the instance.** Short files exist in three groups — `cas9` (one),
`img_` (two), `tuned_s3k` (four). **2026-09-17 is clean**: every set compared against itself is of
uniform length. The morning tuned scans are ragged but the morning log quotes no correlation from
them. Trace/retrace is unaffected everywhere, because `fwd` and `back` share a file and a shape.

### 2.3 The best candidate image is closed, with a mechanism

`sessions/2026-09-17-bench.md` §3.25 has carried the 636-count profile at ±15,000 X as
**UNDETERMINED since 2026-09-17**. It is the feedback loop recovering from the **30,000-count X
flyback** between passes on a surface tilted **−0.105 Z counts per X count**, leaving **3,144
counts** of Z error at every pass start. **Dropping two pixels of twenty-one collapses the
consecutive-pass correlation to nothing.**

**The same arithmetic predicts its absence twice** — the 2D wide images raster without flyback
(predicted small, observed 37 and 78 counts); the three-Y runs fly back but are nearly level
(predicted 28 and 238, observed −99 and +47). **One equation, three data sets.** A falsification,
not a null.

**The check already existed here, one level up.** §3.22 killed an r of +0.75 by dropping the first
**line** of an image. **Nobody applied it to the first pixels of a line.**

### 2.4 Four files were missing from their own directory's index, and that is why a test was thought unrun

§3.25 also records that the ±15,000 place test *"has not been run"*. **It had been.**
`scan_wide_25nm_1.csv` and `_2.csv` are two complete images of the same nine Y places on the same X
grid — same place +0.216 ± 0.228, different place +0.074 ± 0.063, permutation **p = 0.24**.

**They were not in `sessions/data/2026-09-17-bench/README.md`**, along with `line_repeated_wide.csv`
— the candidate itself — and `line_three_y_positions.csv`. **Four files, and between them they are
the strongest candidate and the data that answers it.** Fixed. **The fifth time in this project that
something recorded as unavailable was already here.**

### 2.5 The three-Y runs are minutes apart, not thirty — and the logs held a measurement nobody used

Two subagents said "half an hour" and "nine minutes"; neither is evidenced. §3.14 is timestamped
**03:48**, §3.16 **03:53**, and §3.15 — all four three-Y runs — sits between them. **All four fit in
about five minutes.**

**The first line of every `ycontrol_run*.log` records the onset Z at which the loop found the
surface:** run 1 **44,200**, run 2 **48,600**, run 3 **52,000**, run 4 **50,800**. **Between run 2
and run 4 the gap moved 2,200 counts — about 3% of the Z range, not most of it.**

**This weakens the surviving candidate rather than strengthening it.** If the surface barely moved
across that interval, genuine non-reproduction is the better-supported reading. **It does not close
it**, because **onset Z measures the gap, not lateral position**, and a drift in X or Y would carry
the tip to a different patch of gold while barely changing the Z at which it finds the surface.
**Lateral drift has never been measured in this project.** That is a sharper open question than the
one it replaces, and it changes the settling experiment: **record onset Z at every run.**

### 2.6 The I-V goes as V^1.55, not V²

`sessions/2026-09-17-bench.md` §3.15 reads the round-7 curve as *"roughly V^2"*. Refitting its own
six points gives **V^1.55**, log-log R² 0.985. **A pure V² law predicts 85 nA at 0.5 V against the
31.7 nA measured** — a factor of 2.7. Everything else in that section survives: resistance 58.8 to
15.8 MΩ, and the showcase's "about four times faster than Ohm's law" is 3.73x and correct.

**It did not propagate**: "V^2" appears only in the session log. Every live document says
"superlinear", which is right.

**And the curve establishes a BARRIER, which is not the same claim as a tunnelling gap.** A
contaminant film, a thin oxide or a dirty near-contact are all superlinear too, and 16-59 MΩ sits at
the low end of the range tunnelling occupies. The 2026-09-19 Z test on a **different tip and sample**
found no clean gap. **Neither supersedes the other; the deliverables must say which junction they
mean.**

### 2.7 The gap-motion number is right, and one candidate cause was missing

`release_watch_run1.log` verified line by line — 19,000 → 42,000 → beyond the 62,000 sweep top is
**>43,000 counts in 6.3 s**, and back to 6,000 at 111 s is **>56,000**. There is **no CSV**; it was
lost with three others because the scripts wrote rows only in `finally`.

**The 6.4 s excursion begins 0.1 s after a 60-step motor move ends**, and post-move relaxation was
not among the candidate causes. **It should be.** But the motion **reverses direction and overshoots
its own starting point**, which relaxation does not do, and the 56,000-count figure spans 6.4 s to
111 s.

**Three further candidates came from the photographs** and are now in `STATUS.md`: the **undressed
cables** bridging the suspended platform to the fixed bench (a nonlinear path to ground that can
stick and release — and the cheapest test in the list, hang a known mass and compare the droop with
the cables lifted clear); the **coin mass** standing tall and unrestrained; the
**spring-hook-in-eyebolt joints**. **All are candidates, not evidence.**

## 4. Tooling: `check_facts.py` gained one check and lost three false positives

**Four defects, all found by being hit by them, all fixed with a red-then-green test.**

1. **The failure message named a qualifier that never worked.** It told the reader to add *"a word
   like 'was'"* to excuse a deliberate quotation; `EXCUSES` has only ever matched `was wrong`.
   Anyone following it literally would watch the check fail again with no idea why.
2. **`drops in`, retired for the Keystone standoff, fired on "drops into constant-current mode".**
   Its narrowing was written as *"for the ..."* and the regex accepted only *"as the ..."*, so the
   row was unqualified and matched as a bare substring.
3. **A retired numeric literal matched as the tail of a bigger number** — `800 counts` fired on
   "loop constant 1,800 counts per decade", and `4.096` would have fired inside `14.096`. Fixed with
   a left boundary; tested five ways.
4. **`Images/` and image extensions were in neither alternation of the link checker, so every
   photograph reference in the repository was unchecked** — and `Images/ours/README.md` is one long
   table of them. **Found by the photo pass, which renamed 61 files and then proved the gap by
   re-introducing a broken reference and watching the check stay green.** `Images` and
   `deliverables` added, with `jpg|png|svg|html|csv`.

**A checker that fires on correct lines is a checker people switch off**, which is the failure this
file exists to prevent, arriving from the other direction.

**One blind spot recorded and deliberately NOT patched.** The checker suppresses a hit when the
corrected value appears on the same line, on the sound theory that such a line is a correction
table. **A conflict row names both values by construction** — "ADC full scale 10.24 vs 4.096" — so
the suppression fires whichever value the row declares the winner. That is how §2.8's first entry
survived. Tightening it would cost more than it saves.

## 5. What was NOT done, and what is still unknown

- **No image was produced, and none was found.** 51 recordings searched. Against **201
  reproducibility tests**, a Bonferroni threshold is p < 2.5e-4; nothing reaches it.
- **The gap's motion is characterised at n = 1** at the right timescale. Four CSVs were lost to
  `finally`-only writes.
- **Periodicity could not be tested at all** — the fastest junction record gives a 0.1 Hz floor and
  the gap moves over minutes.
- **Lateral drift has never been measured.** It is now the named gap in the surviving candidate.
- **The Z scale remains inherited from Berard's calibration of a similar disc and unmeasured.** The
  hysteresis result does not depend on it; the counts-per-decade comparison does.
- **The W-Au contact potential is unmeasured**, so electrostatics is not excluded as the cause of the
  junction's behaviour.
- **How the instrument was packed is UNKNOWN**, and three video clips that might show more are over
  the Drive connector's 10 MB cap.
- **Three of the four `d` measurements from photographs disagreed by more than a factor of two.** No
  attempt was made here and none should be.
