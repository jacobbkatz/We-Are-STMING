## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The sample: the whole plate is gold

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"Just added anouther sheet of gold so the enitre sample plate is covered"*.

**Consequence:** the tip lands on gold wherever it lands. 2026-09-17 §3.27 could not tell whether
the junction was on the gold or on the copper tape beside it, and leaned toward copper oxide on the
physics. **That ambiguity is gone.**

> **NOT CONFIRMED, and it is a gate: that the new sheet is electrically continuous to the orange
> bias wire.** The old plate had gold on copper tape which the wire reached. A fresh sheet laid over
> aluminium tape may be joined to nothing — aluminium tape's adhesive usually does not conduct.
> **If the gold does not carry the bias, no tunnelling current can exist.** One meter check.

### 3.2 The tip holder: rebuilt, and half-checked

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"fixed the tip holder"*, then *"new tip holder to standoff is continuos"*.

**What that check proves:** the signal path tip holder → standoff → amplifier input survived the
rebuild. **What it does not prove** is `STATUS.md` safety rule 7, which is the opposite test — the
tip holder must read **OPEN** to the brass piezo electrode. That passed on 2026-09-16 **against the
old holder**, and a rebuild is exactly what can bridge it. **Still owed.**

**Not recorded and asked for:** whether any adhesive was used and which; whether tips can now be
changed without rebuilding; where the tip sits relative to the pivot line.

### 3.3 The suspension: droop 2 mm, f0 11 Hz

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"the drop is maybe 2mm its barley hanging the springs are not very stretchy"*.
Lifted until the springs went slack, then released. **A by-eye figure, not a caliper reading.**

`f0 = 15.76 / sqrt(droop in mm)` = **11.1 Hz**.

| Droop | f0 | Isolates above |
|---|---|---|
| **2 mm — measured** | **11.1 Hz** | **15.8 Hz** |
| 20 mm | 3.5 Hz | 5.0 Hz |
| 62 mm | 2.0 Hz | 2.8 Hz |

**A suspension amplifies at f0 and only isolates above about 1.4 x f0.** Every bit of the
2026-09-17 mechanical noise sits **below 30 Hz and is worst at 5 Hz**. **So the suspension
contributes nothing in the band that matters and amplifies near 11 Hz.**

**To isolate the 5 Hz peak the droop must exceed 20 mm** — ten times softer springs, or ten times
the mass. **The three coin cups cannot do it**: at about 100 g against a requirement of kilograms,
they were sized to trim, not to change f0 tenfold. Berard hung his stack on **2 ft springs** against
our 300 mm (`docs/OTHER_BUILDERS.md` §5).

> **Robust to the one caveat.** *"Barely hanging"* raises the possibility that something else is
> taking weight. **If so the springs stretch less, the droop reads smaller, and the real isolation
> is worse still.** The conclusion holds either way. **Worth checking anyway** — a cable resting on
> the base bypasses the isolation entirely and no spring change fixes that.

### 3.4 d: still unmeasured, and the photograph route is dead

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"its between 1mm and 0mm its really hard to measure"*. **An earlier figure of
5 mm was stated and retracted by Jacob within minutes as a mis-measurement. It was never written
into any document.**

**That range gives the lever ratio as 40 or better** — the design value is its upper bound rather
than the answer. **It does not settle the tunnelling question**, which turns on a sharper threshold:

**The current falls one decade per 50 to 100 Z counts at the onset. Vacuum tunnelling falls one
decade per 0.1 nm. Those meet only if d is under about 0.13 mm**, or 0.065 mm on the shallower
slope. **So this is a threshold test, not a precision measurement.**

**And the two ends of Jacob's range tell opposite stories:**

| If d is | 287-419 count wobble becomes | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **0.13 mm** | **0.6 to 0.8 nm** | Just under the ~1 nm at which a vacuum gap stops conducting. **Tunnelling, and being shaken out of it** |
| **1.0 mm** | **4.4 to 6.5 nm** | Far too large for a gap to survive. **The tip is resting on something** |

**Five photographs were taken and committed** to `Images/ours/`. **Jacob confirmed the layout**: the
TOP and BOTTOM ball ends are the side-by-side pair, so the pivot line is vertical in those frames,
and the motor screw is the one on the RIGHT. **That part is settled.**

**Measuring d from them was tried three times and failed three times:**

| Attempt | Method | Disc diameter |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | By eye, 50 px grid, cropped near the bore | ~425 px |
| 2 | By eye, 100 px grid, whole face | ~210 px |
| 3 | Colour detection of the brass, `numpy` | 687-725 px, inconsistent across four frames |

**A factor of more than two between attempts by the same method.** Abandoned, with four reasons
written into `Images/ours/README.md`: the tip is a soldered structure rather than a point and which
feature is the working tip cannot be told from the image; brass, aluminium and copper all sit in one
hue band under warm light; the balls stand proud toward the camera while the tip is recessed, so the
camera must be square to **half a degree** for the apparent offset to stay under 0.13 mm; and the
threshold is **three pixels** at this scale.

**What replaces it, and it is a minute at the bench:** which feature is the tip, and **how far it
sits from the centre of the brass disc**, by ruler or caliper against the disc rim. Both are at the
same depth so none of the four problems apply, and `docs/FACTS.md` already fixes the disc centre at
**1.000 mm** from the pivot line.

### 3.5 An observation from the photographs, offered as a hypothesis

**The tip is mounted well off the centre of the piezo disc.** That is visible in the frames even
though the distance is not measurable from them.

**A piezo disc deflects most at its centre and not at all at its clamped rim.** So an off-centre tip
gets **less Z throw** than one at the middle, and picks up **tilt as well as displacement**. And
because X, Y and Z all drive the same disc here — DSUB1 carries −(Z±X) and −(Z±Y),
`docs/FACTS.md` — **an off-centre tip would couple X motion into Z**.

> **That is a candidate explanation for the −0.17 counts of Z per count of X tilt** which reproduced
> in **every single scan** on 2026-09-17 and was never explained. **HYPOTHESIS, not a finding.**
> Nothing was measured to support it. **If the holder is rebuilt again, centring the tip on the disc
> is free** and buys maximum Z throw, less X-to-Z coupling, and d fixed at the CAD's 1.000 mm by
> construction rather than by luck.

## 9. Process lessons

- **A fix recorded at the wrap is not a fix in the file.** 2026-09-17 §7 said the bias argument was
  fixed. The literal was still there. **Grep the file for the thing you claimed to change**, at the
  wrap, not the next day.
- **When a measurement disagrees with itself, stop measuring and say so.** Three attempts at d from
  photographs gave three answers a factor of two apart. The fourth attempt was not worth making, and
  the honest negative result plus a one-minute alternative was worth more than a number.
- **A number computed in conversation is not a number in the repository, and that gap is a feature.**
  The 5 mm figure got as far as full arithmetic — lever ratio, nm per step, the wobble in nm, a
  verdict on tunnelling — and was retracted before anything was written down. **Nothing had to be
  unwound.**
- **A test that asserts the wrong kind of failure passes while the fault is present.** The detrend
  check looked for a reversed true positive; a shared tilt fakes a signal instead. **Same shape as
  the 2026-09-09 safety-rule checker.** Ask what the fault actually does before asserting on it.
