## 3. Measurements

### 3.1 The loose gold, and why it explains the snap

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"the seccond session today the gold wasnt as stuck on as the first with whole
gold foil on it was moveing a bit if i blew on it versus it didnt the first session"*, then
*"so maybe it was the gold lifting off when the tip got close becuase it wasnt attached as much as
it was before"*.

**Gold leaf is about 100 nm thick, so it weighs 0.019 Pa.** The gold is on the bias and the tip is a
virtual earth, so the full bias sits across the gap. Electrostatic pressure `0.5·eps0·(V/gap)²`:

| Gap | Pull at 0.5 V | Against the leaf's own weight |
|---|---|---|
| 10 um | 0.011 Pa | 0.6x |
| **1 um** | **1.11 Pa** | **58x** |
| 300 nm | 12.3 Pa | 650x |
| **100 nm** | **111 Pa** | **5,800x** |

**It rises as 1/gap², so it runs away — a pull-in instability, and a pull-in instability is a SNAP.**
`2026-09-18-bench.md` measured under 1,000 counts to over 10,000 in **25 to 75 Z counts**.

> **The blow test is itself the calibration.** A gentle breath is **0.6 to 15 Pa**. A sheet that
> moves when blown on responds to about a pascal — **the same order as the electrostatic pull at a
> micron gap.** The test Jacob ran by eye proves the bias can move this leaf.

**It also covers the rest of that night**, which plate stick-slip covers less well: the onset
wandering with sd **7,548 counts** while the motor was held still; one step moving the onset
**14,000-29,000 counts** while others moved nothing; **retract steps bringing the gold into
contact**; scattered stray contacts at several Z with nothing between; and possibly the **7x**
clear-tip noise, since a large, loose, *moving* conductor beside a 100 MΩ input node modulates
stray capacitance — that log already listed whole-plate gold as an untested noise candidate, and
loose makes it worse.

**Honest limits, recorded in `STATUS.md`.** **Three things changed between the two nights** — the
gold came loose, the holder was rebuilt, and the side screws went in about 7 turns. **Not a clean
single-variable experiment.** The tip is still blunt and bent. **Plate stick-slip is not ruled out
and both can be true.** But it is the only candidate whose mechanism *predicts* a snap rather than
merely being compatible with one, and on 2026-09-17 the gold did not move when blown on and the
instrument held still enough for an eight-round I-V curve, a 90-pass drift series and four scans.

### 3.2 The sample-plate magnets

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"like mechpandas design we have magnets below the copper on the sample bank
becuase his hopg carbon sample came on a magnet plate so we intended to do the same"*.

**They were already in the design files and were never in `docs/INVENTORY.md`.** `docs/BOM.md`
lists **4 small disc magnets "for the sample pocket"**, size UNKNOWN; `CAD/prints/README.md` finds
**four blind pockets in `SamplePlate`, Ø4.200 x 6 mm deep, 4 along Y at X centre**. Now recorded.

**Ruled out by arithmetic:**

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Can they move the gold? | **No, by about 1,000x.** Gold is diamagnetic, χ ≈ −3.4e-5. `(χ/μ0)·B·dB/dz` at **0.5 T and 1,000 T/m** is **7% of the leaf's own weight**, which is itself 58x too small against the bias pull. **Second time this has been asked** — `sessions/2026-09-17-bench.md` §3.1 answered it for why the leaf would not stick, and the answer is the same |
| Can they make the 7x noise? | **No, by 5 orders of magnitude.** A 1 cm² input loop vibrating 10 um at 10 Hz in a 10 T/m gradient induces **6.3e-7 V**, which through 100 MΩ is **0.0063 pA** against a 1 nA signal |

**Still live, both untested:** a DC pull on the **holder's metal stake** if it is ferromagnetic, and
**magnetic stiction between the plate's four magnets and its three steel ball screws** — which is a
concrete mechanism for the stick-slip `2026-09-18-bench.md` hypothesised. **Both got stronger that
night**: the 7-turn hand-set brought the plate about **2.2 mm closer** to the head, and magnetic
force falls off roughly as 1/z³ to 1/z⁴ past a couple of magnet diameters.

### 3.3 The tip is tungsten — and that closes one of them

**`SAID`, Jacob:** *"the tip is tungsten wire"*.

**Tungsten is paramagnetic and very weakly so**, χ ≈ +7.8e-5 — about the size of gold's
diamagnetism with the opposite sign. At 0.5 T and 1,000 T/m the force reaches **16% of the tip's own
weight**, about **15 uN** on a 0.25 x 10 mm wire.

> **But the size is not the decisive part. The shape is.** The magnets are **Ø4.2 mm**, so their
> field varies over **millimetres**, while the approach happens over **microns**. **Across the gap
> range the force is essentially constant — a fixed offset, not a rising one. No runaway, so no
> snap.** The hypothesis is closed.

**Tungsten also weakens "a floppy tip snaps."** Its Young's modulus is about **400 GPa**, roughly
twice steel, so a tungsten tip is far stiffer than the **100 nm** unsupported leaf. **That leaves
the leaf as the compliant thing in the gap, which strengthens §3.1.**

**Still not recorded: what the holder's "metal stake" is made of.** The magnet test still applies
to it and is step 2 of the bench card.

## 9. Process lessons

- **The most valuable thing tonight came from Jacob noticing something and mentioning it, not from
  any measurement.** *"It was moveing a bit if i blew on it"* is worth more than the whole session's
  arithmetic, and the arithmetic only confirmed what he had already guessed. **Ask what looked
  different, not only what was measured.**
- **A casual observation can carry its own calibration.** The blow test set a force scale — about a
  pascal — that turned out to be the same order as the electrostatic pull. Neither of us intended it
  as a measurement.
- **Ruling something out with numbers is worth writing down even when the answer is no.** The magnet
  question has now been asked twice. **The arithmetic is in `STATUS.md` so the third time it is
  asked it takes ten seconds.**
- **One word from the person in the room closed a hypothesis that arithmetic alone left open.**
  Tungsten. Same lesson as 2026-09-18-bench's tip-centring correction, one day running.
