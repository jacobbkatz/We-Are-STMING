### 3.2 Drift cannot explain the loop, and it had to be checked

**A gap closing during a cycle produces a loop of exactly the observed sign**, so this was the
alternative that had to be excluded before the hysteresis meant anything.

| Run | Median cycle | Drift (log §3.11) | Maximum drift contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| −0.1 V | 3.2 s | +29 counts/s | **92 counts** |
| −0.5 V | 2.5 s | +83 counts/s | **207 counts** |
| +0.1 V | 6.1 s | +7 counts/s | **43 counts** |
| +0.5 V | 3.4 s | +22 counts/s | **75 counts** |

**At most 43 to 207 counts against loops of 714 to 1,926.** The log's own drift correction removes
at most about 120. **The loop survives either way.** The cycle durations are recomputed from the
timestamps in the files; the drift rates are the log's.

### 3.3 How long we spent inside the window where tunnelling had to appear

**This is the measurement that answers "could we have blinked and missed it".**

- The window: **20 ADC counts (about 62 pA, just above the 9-19 counts of noise) to 1,000 counts**,
  which is the Z test's own stop target — so every approach really traverses it. **1.70 decades, or
  about 0.17 nm of gap if it were a vacuum gap.**
- **Sampling: 227 readings per second, stepping Z 4 counts at a time.** Both recomputed from the
  raw files.
- **60,928 readings were taken inside that window, across 109 separate approaches** — a median of
  364 to 1,180 per approach.
- **A vacuum gap crosses that window in 10 to 22 Z counts.** The nearest we came was about 1,500.

**On the motor we are blind by construction**: one motor step moves the tip at least **1.94 nm** at
the top of `d`'s bound, which is **eleven times the whole window**. There is no motor position
inside it. **On the piezo we are not blind, and we looked.**

## 13. The checker's escape hatch, fixed

**§12 recorded this and Jacob asked for it fixed rather than logged.**

`check_facts.py` exempted a **whole line** if any correction word — *retired*, *corrected*,
*superseded* — appeared anywhere on it, and it did that **before looking at which value matched**.
Retiring the register's row count caught **one stale copy out of six**: the other five sat on wide
table rows containing the phrase *"a RETIRED table"*, which names the mechanism and says nothing
about the number 200 characters further along the same row.

**Two changes, both in `Code/pc/check_facts.py`:**

1. **The excuse is now looked for near the value it excuses** — within 140 characters of where the
   retired literal actually sits, rather than anywhere on the line.
2. **An occurrence that is only naming the mechanism does not count.** *"a RETIRED table"*, *"the
   retired values"*, *"the superseded column"* are the name of a thing, not a statement that the
   number beside them is out of date.

**Proved both ways.** The exact line that slipped through was planted back into
`AI_WORKFLOW_SECTION.md`, phrasing and all: **the checker exits 1 and names it.** Restored, it
exits 0.

**It also found five lines nobody had looked at** — `sessions/README.md` summarises what each
session found, in that session's own terms, and five of those summaries quote a figure that has
since been retired. **The findings were not rewritten.** A marker sits beside each one saying it

### 14.2 The figures were unreadable on a phone. Measured, not guessed.

Jacob asked for the figures to be made more readable. A render at a 390 px viewport put the
number on it: **fig03's footer came out at about 4 CSS px.** A grey smear. Three separate causes,
all fixed:

1. **The type was too small for the reduction.** The four sizes were 13.5 / 10.5 / 9.5 / 8.5 pt.

2. **The footers were setting the width of every image.** `savefig` writes with

3. **A phone cannot show a ten-inch chart, at any type size.** Below 760 px the website now keeps

### 14.3 What the bigger type broke, and the checker that found it

Raising the type broke **twelve of the thirteen figures at once** — footers straight through their
own x-axis labels, panel titles through the row below's tick labels, annotation blocks into each
other. Finding those by eye took four rounds of rendering and looking, which is exactly the kind
of thing this project keeps paying for twice.

**`deliverables/2026-09-19-pause/figures/code/check_layout.py`** now does it in one pass: it draws
every figure, asks the renderer for the bounding box of every piece of text, and reports any pair
that overlaps. **Proved red then green** — fig09's line height was set back to a deliberately
wrong value and the checker named six colliding pairs; restored, it passes.

Two things it got wrong first, both worth writing down:

- **It counted text that is never drawn.** matplotlib keeps a `Text` for every tick it has ever
  placed, including ticks outside the current view limits, and those still report a bounding box.
  Most of a run's findings were phantom ticks.
- **It counted empty space as ink.** Every line gets a box `fontsize × linespacing` tall, so a
  single 12 pt line at the house linespacing of 1.6 reports a box half again as tall as its
  glyphs. That made fig08's ladder rows look like collisions when they are seventeen pixels apart
  on the page.

`fit_footer()` also had to be rewritten. The first version made room by **compressing the axes**,
and on fig12 it squeezed a chart into a tenth of its height to fit a nine-line footer. Squeezing
the picture to fit the caption is the wrong way round. It now **adds paper at the bottom of the
figure** and leaves every axes the same physical size — which costs nothing, because a browser
