## 2. The DAC configuration loss: a real lead at last

Zoomed into schematic page 1 at the four AD5761 symbols. **Every one of them carries a green
no-connect cross on pins 2, 3, 9, 10 and 11:**

| Pin | Name | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | **CLEAR#** | active-low input, **floating** |
| 3 | **RESET#** | active-low input, **floating** |
| 10 | SDO | no DAC readback — consistent with H1 carrying no MISO |
| 11 | LDAC# | floating, but unused: the firmware writes with `CMD_WR_UPDATE_DAC_REG` |
| 9 | DNC | correct, do-not-connect |

**Why this is the best hypothesis so far.** A floating CMOS input sits at an undefined potential
and can be driven low by coupled noise alone. Four of them, on one board, in one noise
environment, would glitch **together** — which is the defining feature of this fault and the thing
the rail-dip and thermal theories never explained. A RESET# assertion would clear the control
register, kill the outputs, and be undone by `RSET`. That is the symptom exactly.

**Two things must be checked before believing it:**

1. **Does the AD5761R have internal pull-ups on CLEAR# and RESET#?** If yes, floating is far less
   dangerous and the hypothesis weakens a lot. **Datasheet question. Nobody has looked.**
2. **Are they actually open on our board?** Meter continuity from U1 pin 2 and pin 3 to 3.3 V.
   Thirty seconds with a continuity beeper.

**If confirmed, the fix is four short wires** tying CLEAR# and RESET# to 3.3 V — a permanent fix
rather than the periodic re-arm workaround discussed on 2026-09-01.

## 3. Ranking the 37 nA candidates by impedance

The preamp's input is a **virtual ground**. For surface leakage to inject current there, the
current is whatever voltage sits at the far end of the path, divided by the path resistance:

| Source at the far end | Resistance needed for 37 nA | Plausible as a surface path? |
|---|---|---|
| A ±15 V supply rail | **405 MOhm** | **Yes.** Very typical of a contaminated surface |
| A ~0.5 V galvanic cell on the shield | **13 MOhm** | **Low.** That is a poor insulator, not a dirty one |

**This favours the cyanoacrylate and flux hypotheses over the floating shield** as the source of
the DC offset. It does not clear the shield — a floating shield is a genuine noise problem and
still needs grounding — but the arithmetic says a rail leaking to the input node across a dirty
surface is the more likely offset mechanism.

**The recommendation does not change.** Ground the shield first: it is one reversible wire, and
the rebuild consumes the only spare board.

**But a new and cheaper test now exists.** If the offset is rail-to-input leakage it should
**scale with rail voltage**. Drop the supply from ±15 V to ±10 V and the offset should fall by
about a third. If it does not move at all, the rails are not the source and the ranking above is
wrong.

### 10.2 The preamp gerbers embed their own netlist

- **The preamp's supply nets are named `+9V` and `-9V`.** We run it at ±15 V, which the OPA627

### 15.1 Block A1 — the DAC CLEAR# and RESET# pins

Measured on the chip legs directly. There is no other option: `NET_7` and `NET_8` are single-pad
nets, so the TSSOP leg is the only place those signals physically exist on the board.

**Controls, run first:**

| Control | Points | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Meter self-test | probe tips together | **beep** |
| C1 | U1 pin 15 to U3 pin 15 (both 3.3 V) | **beep** |
| C2 | U1 pin 5 to U1 pin 16 (both AGND) | **beep** |

## 16. Work done

**The shield was stripped and rebuilt.** Not in the plan as written — Block C was rewritten during
the session once the measurements above showed its premise was wrong.

1. Aluminium tape **completely removed** from the box
2. Re-covered in **copper tape only**

**Consequences:**

- The **copper/aluminium galvanic couple is eliminated by construction**, not by measurement. There
  is no longer a dissimilar metal in the assembly for the copper to pair with.
- Soldered seams should make the shield one conductor, so a single ground bond now grounds the
  whole thing rather than one patch.

> **VERIFY, and do this before anything else next session.** The acceptance test was not run or not
> recorded. **Every point on the shield must beep to the ground wire**, including the far corner
> and across every seam. Until that passes, Block D3 still cannot be trusted.

## 17. New faults

### The shield was in a worse state than either document described

Not "floating" (2026-09-01) and not "grounded" — **partially grounded and discontinuous**, which is
arguably the worst of the three. Grounded metal sat directly against floating metal, millimetres
from the highest-impedance node in the instrument, with the floating patches still free to collect
charge and couple against their grounded neighbours.

### The trap this set for Block D3, caught before it was sprung

Block D3 says: ground the shield, re-measure, and if it still reads near 29873 counts then
**"the shield was not the cause — the board rebuild is next."** The rebuild is irreversible and
consumes the only spare board.

With a discontinuous shield, D3 would have been testing **"one patch of the shield grounded"**.
It could have returned "no improvement" even if the floating shield genuinely were the problem —
and that false negative would have sent the project into consuming the spare board for nothing.

**Found with a continuity beeper on a dead board, for the cost of thirty seconds.**

Both were introduced by the remote session. Neither would have been caught by anyone who had not
walked the procedure slowly with the hardware in front of them.

## 27. Process lessons

**Two people independently doing the same analysis is not free.** A day of remote work went into
re-deriving something already committed. The cost was not the duplication — it was that the second
pass was **shallower** and would have left "pin 4 is unrouted" out of the bench reference entirely.

**Nuh's control measurements are the model for this project.** The expected result on the DAC pins
was "no beep", which is indistinguishable from a probe slipping off a 0.65 mm leg. He proved he
could contact a leg *before* trusting eight opens. The 2026-08-31 session lost an hour for want of
exactly that discipline, and this session recorded the lesson as a rule.

**A verbal finding stood unmeasured for five days and shaped three sessions of planning.** "The
shield was never grounded" was marked honestly on 2026-09-01 as *reported from the bench, not
measured* — and then cited as fact everywhere afterwards, including in a bench plan built on it.
**The marking was correct and nobody acted on it.**

That is a bigger source of lost time in this project than wrong measurements or bad reasoning.

## 29. The specific defect in `CLAUDE.md`

Section 3 ranked `docs/PROJECT_HANDOFF_SUMMARY.md` **last** and said "never cite the handoff
against a newer document". Correct about conflicts. But it trained the reader to treat "ranked
last" as "not worth opening" — and the handoff's **header banner is where corrections were being
written**, including Nuh's JP1 finding.

> **Precedence is a property of a document. Freshness is a property of a passage.**
> A correction banner at the top of an old file can be the newest text in the repository.

## 30. What changed

**`CLAUDE.md` §3 rewritten.** Precedence order kept, but now says explicitly that a low-ranked
document is still **read** — it is only outranked when it **disagrees**. The handoff's body is
superseded; **its header is a live correction log and is read first.** The JP1 duplication is cited
as the worked example, including that the second pass was *shallower*.

**`CLAUDE.md` §3b added — "Before you record anything as unknown, search for it."** Mandatory
greps before writing UNKNOWN, VERIFY, "needs a bench test", or starting any derivation from
scratch. Plus a table of what worked for each hard-to-read file type, because **"I could not open
it" is not "it does not contain the answer"** — the schematic needed a third tool before one
worked. And a rule to say so, and credit the author, when a finding turns out to be already
recorded.

**`docs/INDEX.md` created — the mechanical half of the fix.** Zips, PDFs and CAD files are not
greppable; this lists what is inside every one of them, with the extraction command that works.
It also carries a table of **answers already extracted, so they are not re-derived**, and it flags
the stale `START_HERE_gotchas.md` / `README.md` / `BOM.md` copies buried in
`We-Are-STMING_PCB.zip` as **not to be read**.

**The handoff's own banner now says what it is** — a live correction log to be read even though the
file is ranked last.
