## 2. New finding: the preamp case shield is floating

**Reported from the bench, not measured today.**

The preamp enclosure is shielded with **copper tape and aluminium tape**, and **the shielding was
never bonded to ground.** Described at the time as the case behaving like "an ungrounded battery".

Two mechanisms follow, both of which are **inference and need testing**, not established fact:

1. **Galvanic cell.** Copper and aluminium in contact, with atmospheric humidity as the
   electrolyte, form a genuine galvanic couple. Dissimilar-metal EMF of a few hundred millivolts
   is ordinary. That is a DC source sitting on the enclosure with no defined potential.

2. **Floating shield.** An ungrounded conductive enclosure is capacitively coupled to everything
   around it and to the input node, with no fixed reference. **A floating shield can be worse
   than no shield**, because it collects charge and then couples it straight into the highest
   impedance node in the circuit.

### Why this matters for the 37 nA

Order-of-magnitude arithmetic, **not a measurement**: to drive 37 nA, an EMF of 0.5 to 1 V needs
a path of roughly 13 to 27 MΩ. That is entirely plausible for a surface leakage path across a
contaminated or humid board.

So this sits **alongside** the cyanoacrylate hypothesis from 2026-08-31, not instead of it. Both
produce a settled DC offset rather than a ramp, which is consistent with everything measured on
2026-08-31. Nothing measured so far distinguishes them.

### The practical consequence, which is the important part

> **If the floating shield is the cause, rebuilding the board will not fix it**, because the new
> board goes into the same ungrounded box.

The acceptance test in `sessions/2026-08-31-results.md` section 6 has a branch reading "settles
near 29873 again → not contamination, rethink from scratch". **This finding is the leading
candidate for that branch.**

Recommended order, so the two causes can be told apart:

1. **Ground the shield first**, on the existing board, before rebuilding anything. It is a single
   wire and it is reversible.
2. **Re-run the acceptance capture** with the shield bonded. If the offset drops substantially,
   the shield was at least part of it, and that is learned without consuming the spare board.
3. **Then rebuild** if it is still bad.

This is deliberately the opposite order to the 2026-08-31 plan, on the grounds that the cheap
reversible test should come before the irreversible one.

### Build rules that follow

- **Bond the shield to circuit ground, at one point only.** One point, not several — multiple
  bonds around an enclosure create a ground loop, which trades one noise problem for another.
- **Solder the ground wire to the copper tape.** Aluminium tape cannot be soldered because of its
  oxide layer, which is already recorded in `docs/START_HERE_gotchas.md`.
- **Do not leave copper and aluminium tape in contact with each other.** The existing gotchas note
  says they are not interchangeable because of solderability. This session adds a second and
  independent reason: in contact they are a galvanic couple.
- Aluminium tape's adhesive is usually non-conductive, so **overlapping strips may not be
  connected to each other at all.** Continuity across the shield needs checking with a meter
  rather than assuming.

**VERIFY:** whether the copper and aluminium tape are actually in contact anywhere on the
existing box, and whether the shield has continuity across its whole area. Both are meter checks.

### The DAC range error, recorded because of how it happened

`CLAUDE.md` was written this session stating X and Y as **±5 V**, taken from the firmware source
comments. That is wrong. They are **±3 V**.

The proof does not need a meter:

- X, Y and bias are all constructed with **identical mode bits**, `0b0000000000000101`. Identical
  bits cannot produce different output ranges.
- Bias was **measured** at ±3 V at the sample holder on 2026-08-31.
- Therefore X and Y are ±3 V.

`PROJECT_HANDOFF_SUMMARY.md` A.4.10 already said exactly this, including the sentence "Firmware
comments claiming ±5 V for X and Y are wrong". It was missed.

**Why it matters at the bench:** believing ±5 V means believing the scan range is 67% larger than
it is. Every X and Y travel figure derived from it would be wrong.

**Why it is worth recording as a process note:** this is precisely the failure the document
precedence rule in `CLAUDE.md` exists to prevent — trusting a source that a newer document had
already corrected. The rule was written and then broken in the same session. Corrected in
`CLAUDE.md`, `README.md`, `docs/WIRING.md`, and flagged in `STATUS.md` so the stale comment in the
firmware does not catch anyone again.

Two items from the previous session were re-confirmed today as correct and are **not** in dispute:

- The OPA627 feedback loop is **closed**. It settles rather than ramping. The old handoff's
  "open feedback loop" claim remains wrong.
- A person within a metre of the preamp injects 20 to 50 nA. Recorded 2026-08-31 s3.2.

A fix for the DAC configuration loss was discussed and **not implemented**. The proposal was a
periodic `RSET` every 30 minutes. Reading the source, that would also **slam Z to a rail**, zero
the bias, and zero the step counter, because `AD5761::reset()` sends a full software reset and
`STM::reset()` rebuilds the status struct. On a timer, with the tip engaged, that is a scheduled
tip crash. The safer version — re-write `CMD_WR_CTRL_REG` and re-send the last commanded value,
restoring state rather than zeroing it — was described but deliberately not written, since neither
helps mid-scan and neither **detects** anything. The real fix is wiring the AD5761 ALERT pins to
spare Teensy GPIOs, which is what would make the fault visible to software at all.

**Do not do:**

- Do not rebuild the preamp before grounding the shield and retesting. The cheap reversible test
  comes first.
- The standing rules in `STATUS.md` all still apply.

## 7. Process lessons

1. **The precedence rule was written and then broken within the same session.** `CLAUDE.md` was
   authored stating that newer documents win, and the ±5 V DAC figure was then taken from a source
   comment that a newer document had already corrected. Writing the rule down is not the same as
   following it. Check the correction table before quoting any number out of the firmware.
2. **Things buried in archives may as well not exist.** `WHICH_SCANHEAD_PART.txt` contained "wrap
   it in copper tape and ground it at one point" — the advice that turned out to matter for the
   fault currently blocking the build — and it sat unread inside a zip. If it matters, it belongs
   in a file GitHub will show.
3. **Check before deleting, not after.** `our_preamp_cad_files/` was removed as a presumed
   duplicate and restored a minute later when a byte comparison showed the files differ. The
   comparison costs one command and should have come first.
4. **A proposed fix deserves the same scrutiny as a finding.** "Just `RSET` every 30 minutes"
   sounds harmless and reads as harmless, until you follow it into `AD5761::reset()` and find it
   zeroes the outputs, the bias and the step counter. Read the source before agreeing to a
   workaround.
