## 3. Measurements

**None taken at the bench.** Two numbers were computed, and one repository dimension was read
from the mesh rather than guessed.

### 3.1 Will graphite carry the bias? Yes, by about eleven orders of magnitude

Asked by Jacob before buying. Computed rather than asserted.

| Quantity | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Electrode graphite resistivity | **7 to 20 µΩ·m** | Web, several grades. Worst case taken |
| Resistance of a 14 mm x 2 mm disk, worst path, edge to edge | **about 0.01 Ω** | rho x L / A, L = 14 mm, A = 28 mm^2 |
| Resistance of the tunnelling gap at −0.5 V and 1 nA | **500 MΩ** | Ohm's law |
| Ratio | **the graphite is about 50 billion times smaller** | |
| Voltage lost inside the graphite at 1 nA | **about 10 pV out of 500 mV** | |

**Conclusion: bulk conductivity is a non-issue for any graphite sample.** Even a 1 kΩ clip contact
would lose one microvolt out of half a volt. **Conduction was never a reason to reject graphite.**

### 3.2 What actually rejects machined graphite: the Z range

| Quantity | Value | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Z full travel | **about 680 nm** | **INFERRED** from Berard's disc, not ours — `docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md` line 219. `STATUS.md` notes the real figure may be far smaller |
| Machined electrode graphite finish | **around 1 µm**, grain 5 to 20 µm | Typical values, **not measured** |

**The peak-to-valley roughness of machined graphite is larger than the entire Z range.** The piezo
physically cannot follow it. That is a mechanical limit, not a resolution one.

### 3.3 Sample plate size, read from the mesh

**`SamplePlate` is 12.00 x 70.00 x 49.00 mm** (`CAD/prints/README.md` line 45). A standard
75 x 25 mm microscope slide overhangs the long edge by 5 mm and is otherwise a comfortable fit.

## 6. False trails, and one live candidate

### The graphite block was a weak recommendation and was withdrawn within the session

Claude suggested a cheap graphite block as a sacrificial first-contact target, then withdrew it
once Jacob produced the actual listings. **Reasons, none of them conduction:**

- **Delivery.** The 10x10x10 mm cube at $6.49 ships **September 29 to October 8** — two to three
  weeks against a two-day constraint. The 14x2 mm disks, 5 for $4.89, arrive **Sunday 20 September**.
  **Neither meets two days.**
- **The copper tape already on the sample plate does the same job for free.** For "does a current
  appear when the tip touches something", copper tape is as good as graphite.
- **Electrode graphite sheds conductive dust**, and the preamp input node is an open-air node on a
  PTFE standoff that this project has spent weeks de-contaminating.

**Listing trap recorded for whoever revisits it:** that cube's title reads *"Graphite Electrode
Plate Board 10x10x0.4mm"* while the variant priced at $6.49 is **10x10x10mm**, a solid cube. On a
multi-variant listing the title is generic. **Read the Size box, not the title.**

### Live candidate: the bias may never reach the surface the tip sees

**Mechanism.** `docs/ENGINEERING_REFERENCE.md` §3 already warns that non-conductive adhesive
"reintroduces the gap" — **for the shield.** The same property decides the bias path. The sample
plate is printed PETG-CF, an insulator. If the orange bias wire has to cross the tape's adhesive
to reach the copper face the tip lands on, **the surface is floating.**

**Why it fits the evidence exactly.** A floating sample gives no current at any Z, at any motor
position, with a clean touch check and no leak — which is the whole of
`sessions/2026-09-16-bench.md` §3.9 and §3.11.

**Why it was never caught.** The two checks run that night were **sample plate to ground, silent**
and **sample plate to tip, silent.** Both are tests for the ABSENCE of a connection. **Neither
tested that the bias is PRESENT at the copper surface.**

**Not a finding.** How the orange wire attaches to the plate **is not documented anywhere in this
repository**, and no photograph was read to guess it — see the 2026-09-16 photo-reading error.

### Ruled out as sample options

| Option | Why not |
|---|---|
| **Silver foil** | Tarnishes to silver sulfide in air within days; sulfide is an insulator. Crumples like leaf and then dies |
| **Carbon fibre** | It does tunnel — carbon fibres are on the published list of STM-able materials — but a fibre is a **round cylinder about 7 µm across.** The tip must land on its crown. Worst possible geometry for an approach that already cannot find the surface |
| **Pure gold coin** | Die-struck surface, rough far above atomic scale, at bullion price. Berard mounts foil **on** a coin; he does not image the coin |
| **Gold plated onto foil** | The plating is sub-micron and follows the rolled copper underneath. **It is a copper surface wearing a gold coat, and it still crumples.** Exists (Stanford Advanced Materials) but is a specialist order |
| **Template-stripped gold** | The best surface available — atomically flat Au(111) — at **$1,866.95** for MSE's 10 x 10 mm chips. Fisher lists the Platypus equivalent as discontinued. Ruled out on price |

## 8. Next session

**The bench list at the top of `docs/NEXT_SESSION_PLAN.md` is unchanged in substance.** One check
is added and it is cheap, so it goes first.

1. **Two beeps on the copper tape, powered off, BEFORE anything else.**
   - **Orange bias wire to the copper tape's top face, near where the tip lands. This MUST beep.**
     If it does not, that is the null result explained.

     copper face**, not through the tape's back.

## 9. Process lessons

- **A purchase question was answered by arithmetic, not by opinion.** Jacob asked whether graphite
  would conduct the bias. The answer is eleven orders of magnitude of margin, and computing it took
  one calculation. `CLAUDE.md` §3c asks for exactly this before an instruction; it applies just as
  well before a purchase.
- **The most useful finding of the session came from an offhand remark.** *"We only have copper
  tape"* was said to answer a different question, and it opened the adhesive hypothesis. **Jacob and
  Nuh hold facts about the room that no design file contains** — `CLAUDE.md` §3d — and those facts
  arrive sideways, in passing, not in answer to the question that needed them.
- **Two checks for silence are not a check for presence.** Plate-to-ground silent and plate-to-tip
  silent both confirm the absence of a connection. **Neither can confirm that the bias arrives.**
  Worth a look at any other place in this project where a pass is recorded as silence.
